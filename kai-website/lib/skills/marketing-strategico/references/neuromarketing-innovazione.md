# Neuromarketing & Innovation Frameworks

## PART A — CONSUMER PSYCHOLOGY AVANZATA

### 1. System 1 vs System 2 (Kahneman)

#### 1.1 I Due Sistemi
- **System 1 (Fast)**: automatico, emotivo, intuitivo, associativo, parallelo — 95% decisioni consumatore
- **System 2 (Slow)**: controllato, razionale, logico, sequenziale — attivato da sforzo, novità, conflitto

#### 1.2 Implicazioni Marketing
- La maggior parte delle decisioni avviene in System 1 → brand, simboli, heuristics contano più della persuasione razionale
- Il packaging, il colore, il logo sono System 1
- Le comparazioni tecniche, i trade-off sono System 2
- Brand strong = scorciatoia cognitiva per System 1

#### 1.3 Cognitive Load e Decision Fatigue
- Ogni decisione consuma "willpower" cognitivo
- Decision fatigue → preferenza per default, status quo, scelte semplici
- Implicazione: minimizzare friction in checkout, UX, onboarding

### 2. Prospect Theory (Kahneman-Tversky)

#### 2.1 Value Function Asimmetrica
- Le perdite pesano ~2.25x guadagni equivalenti (loss aversion)
- Reference-dependent: valutazione relativa a un reference point
- Diminishing sensitivity: rendimenti decrescenti in entrambe le direzioni

#### 2.2 Endowment Effect
- Possedere qualcosa aumenta il suo valore percepito (+100-200%)
- Applicazione: free trial, "possesso psicologico", "it's yours to keep"
- Rischio: resistenza al cambiamento (switching friction)

#### 2.3 Framing Effects
- "95% survival rate" vs "5% mortality rate" — stesso dato, impatto diverso
- Gain frame vs Loss frame: consumer risk-averse in gain, risk-seeking in loss
- Applicazione pricing: "save €50" (gain) vs "don't lose €50" (loss — più potente)

### 3. Cialdini's 7 Principles of Influence

#### 3.1 Reciprocity (Reciprocità)
- Dare prima di chiedere
- Unexpected + personalized = maggiore reciprocity
- Esempi: campioni gratuiti, contenuto gratuito, concessioni ("door in the face")

#### 3.2 Commitment & Consistency
- Le persone cercano coerenza con i loro impegni precedenti
- "Foot in the door" technique: piccola richiesta → grande richiesta
- Public commitment più potente di private
- Applicazione: micro-yes in funnel, opt-in progressivo

#### 3.3 Social Proof
- Seguiamo il comportamento degli altri (specialmente simili)
- Wisdom of crowds (recensioni, rating, bestseller list)
- Applicazione: "X persone hanno comprato", testimonial, case study, UGC
- Warning: social proof negativo funziona anche (evitare "non fare X come tutti")

#### 3.4 Authority
- Expertise, credibility, status aumentano compliance
- Uniformi, titoli, credenziali, endorsement experts
- Applicazione: "medici raccomandano", celebrity expert, journalistic awards

#### 3.5 Liking
- Siamo più persuasi da chi ci piace (simile, attraente, che ci compiace)
- Applicazione: influencer match con audience, personalizzazione, user similarity
- Halo effect: attributo positivo → percezione positiva generale

#### 3.6 Scarcity
- FOMO drives decision
- "Only 3 left", "Limited time", exclusive access
- Loss frame attivato (prospect theory)
- Warning: se fake, distrugge trust

#### 3.7 Unity (aggiunto 2016)
- Identità condivisa ("we-ness")
- Comunità, tribe, appartenenza
- Applicazione: brand community, insider language, rituals

### 4. Behavioral Biases in Marketing (30+ bias)

#### 4.1 Anchoring
- Primo numero visto diventa riferimento
- MSRP, original price crossed-out, bid anchor
- Potente specialmente quando numero è incerto/sconosciuto

#### 4.2 Default Effect / Status Quo Bias
- Opt-out > opt-in significativamente (Johnson & Goldstein organ donation 86% vs 4%)
- Applicazione: pre-selezione best option, auto-renewal
- Inerzia decisionale: cambio richiede sforzo e decisione conscia

#### 4.3 Bandwagon / Herd Behavior
- Crescente popolarità attira ancora più persone
- Amplificato in social media era
- Cascata informativa: seguo gli altri perché hanno informazione che io non ho

#### 4.4 Confirmation Bias
- Cerchiamo info che confermano credenze esistenti
- Evitiam info contraddittorie
- Implicazione: targeting per beliefs, echo chamber, need for diversified sources

#### 4.5 Availability Heuristic
- Giudichiamo probabile ciò che è vividamente ricordabile
- News, storie memorabili distorcono risk assessment
- Applicazione: storytelling > statistiche

#### 4.6 Representativeness Heuristic
- Giudichiamo per somiglianza a stereotipi
- Es. "looks like an entrepreneur" vs actual data
- Ignoriamo base rate (prior probability)

#### 4.7 Sunk Cost Fallacy
- Persevero perché ho già investito
- Applicazione retention: "tu hai già accumulato X status, non perderlo"
- Irrazionale: costo passato non deve influenzare decisione futura

#### 4.8 Hyperbolic Discounting
- Preferiamo reward immediati vs futuri (discount rate non-exponential)
- "Now" bias: oggi sconto molto, domani sconto poco
- Applicazione: instant gratification, immediate benefit framing

#### 4.9 Choice Paralysis (Barry Schwartz)
- Troppe opzioni → nessuna scelta
- Jam study: 24 flavor stand vs 6 flavor stand — 6 vendeva 10x
- Applicazione: curate, simplify, default selection
- Cognitive overload: paralysis by analysis

#### 4.10 Decoy Effect (Asymmetric Dominance)
- Aggiungere opzione dominated sposta scelta tra le altre
- Classico The Economist: print-only €125, online-only €59, print+online €125 — nessuno prende online-only ma sposta scelta verso combo
- Pricing strategico: decoy come pricing tactic

#### 4.11 Peak-End Rule
- Memoria non è media dell'esperienza
- Ricordo = peak (migliore o peggiore momento) + end (come finisce)
- Applicazione: fine positiva, memorable moments durante customer journey

#### 4.12 IKEA Effect
- Valore percepito aumenta se ho partecipato alla creazione
- Sforzo investito → attachment emotivo
- Applicazione: customization, assembly, DIY experience

#### 4.13 Mere Exposure
- Familiarità aumenta preferenza (solo se valence non è negativa)
- Ripetizione aumenta liking
- Applicazione: frequency in advertising, brand awareness campaign

#### 4.14 Reciprocal Concessions
- Door-in-the-face: grande richiesta rifiutata → richiesta minore accettata
- Reciprocity per concessions, non solo favori

#### 4.15 Contrast Effect
- Oggetto sembra più grande/piccolo vicino ad altri
- Ordine di presentazione delle opzioni conta
- Applicazione: prezzi premium dopo standard, show worst first

#### 4.16 Halo Effect
- Attributo positivo → percezione positiva generale
- Attrattività fisica → intelligenza percepita, credibilità
- Applicazione: celebrity endorsement, brand halo, leadership perception

#### 4.17 Ben Franklin Effect
- Se ti chiedo un favore → tu pensi che tu mi apprezzi di più (instead of me apprezzi te)
- Paradosso: dopo favore, ti piace di più colui che hai aiutato
- Applicazione: ask for feedback/favor per aumentare engagement

#### 4.18 Bizarreness Effect
- Memoria di stimoli strani/unusual è superiore
- Distinctiveness memoriale
- Applicazione: unusual advertising, memorable creative

#### 4.19 Illusion of Control
- Sovrastimiamo capacità di controllare esiti casuali/incerti
- Applicazione: gamification (sense of control anche quando outcome randomico)

#### 4.20 Framing
- Stesso outcome presentato come guadagno vs perdita → scelte diverse
- Loss avoidance più forte che gain seeking
- Applicazione: sempre presentare come "avoid loss" quando goal è behavior change

#### 4.21 Regret Aversion
- Paura di pentirsi dell'azione intrapresa
- Status quo preserva da regret (no blame if nothing changes)
- Applicazione: minimize risk perception, highlight reversibility

#### 4.22 Optimism Bias
- Overestimate positive outcomes per se stessi
- Underestimate risks personali
- Applicazione: messaging about benefits works, but disclaimer about risks meno efficace

#### 4.23 Dunning-Kruger Effect
- Incompetenti overestimate competenza (lack metacognition)
- Esperti underestimate competenza (sanno quanto non sanno)
- Implicazione: targeting deve considerare self-perception vs actual knowledge

#### 4.24 Endowed Progress
- Perceived head start aumenta completion probability
- "Collect 8 stamps to free coffee" vs "Collect 10, get 2 free" stesso valore percepito, ma primo ha pre-filled 2
- Applicazione: progress bar, loyalty program framing, start with 20% complete

#### 4.25 Weber's Law
- Cambiamento percepito proporzionale a baseline (log scale)
- +€1 su €10 ≠ +€1 su €100 (percepito)
- Applicazione: discount framing relativo, percentage vs absolute

#### 4.26 Saliency Bias
- Events récenti, visible, emozionali sono overweighted
- Hot/vivid info > cold/statistical
- Applicazione: emotional testimonial > average rating

#### 4.27 Attribution Bias
- Attribuiamo successo a fattori interni (ability), fallimento a esterni (luck)
- Self-serving bias: io succeedo per me, fallisco per circostanze
- Altrui: loro falliscono per incompetenza, succedono per circostanze
- Applicazione: messaging per success, don't blame customer

#### 4.28 Clustering Illusion
- Vediamo patterns in random data
- Applicazione: small sample fallacy in testing, need proper statistical power

#### 4.29 False Consensus Effect
- Overestimate how many persone agree con noi
- "Everyone thinks..."
- Applicazione: avoid, meglio "many people" con number

#### 4.30 Negative Bias
- Negative info > positive info (2:1 o 3:1 weight)
- Una review negativa ≠ three positive reviews
- Applicazione: prioritize complaint resolution, response to negative feedback critical

### 5. Neuromarketing Research Methods

#### 5.1 Biometric Tools
- **Eye tracking**: fissazioni, saccadi, heatmap, AOI (Area of Interest)
  - Cosa guarda, per quanto tempo, in che ordine
  - Applicazione: packaging test, website UX, advertisement copy
  - Warning: guardo ≠ compro (high attention non sempre correlato con purchase)

- **Facial coding (FACS)**: 43 muscoli facciali codificati in Action Units, mappati a 7 emozioni base (Ekman)
  - Automatic vs controlled expression
  - Applicazione: advertisement testing, brand reaction testing, authenticity assessment
  - Resolution: micro-expressions (1/25 sec) vs macro (more conscious)

- **GSR (Galvanic Skin Response)**: conduttanza pelle → arousal (non valenza)
  - Emotional engagement, not direction
  - Applicazione: quando consumatore engaged (positivo o negativo)
  - Warning: non distingue between excited e scared

- **Heart Rate Variability (HRV)**: stress/relaxation spectrum
  - Low HRV = stress/cognitive load
  - High HRV = relaxation/engagement
  - Applicazione: user experience testing, cognitive load assessment

- **EEG**: attività cerebrale 8-80 Hz bandwidths
  - **Alpha**: relaxation (8-12 Hz)
  - **Beta**: cognitive effort (12-30 Hz)
  - **Theta**: emotion, memory consolidation (4-8 Hz)
  - **Gamma**: binding (>30 Hz)
  - Frontal asymmetry: left > right = approach motivation (positive), right > left = avoidance (negative)
  - Applicazione: advertisement testing, concept evaluation
  - Advantage: temporal resolution high, can detect micro-changes
  - Disadvantage: spatial resolution poor, need many electrodes

- **fMRI**: blood oxygenation level dependent (BOLD) imaging
  - Spatial resolution excellent, temporal poor (~2 sec lag)
  - Regions: amygdala (emotion), prefrontal (decision), nucleus accumbens (reward)
  - Applicazione: pre-launch concept testing, understand deep drivers
  - Warning: costoso, limited throughput, prone to reverse inference fallacy

- **Voice analytics**: pitch, speed, pauses, energy in speech
  - Emotion recognition, stress detection
  - Applicazione: customer service interaction analysis, interview authenticity

#### 5.2 Applications per Stage

- **Concept testing (pre-launch)**: 
  - fMRI/EEG on critical concepts
  - What brain regions activate
  - Predict commercial success (weak-moderate correlation)

- **Packaging design**: 
  - Eye tracking shelf test (actual retail environment)
  - Which elements noticed, how long
  - Gaze path on package

- **Advertisement testing**: 
  - Facial coding + GSR + EEG (vedi neuroscience copy testing in comunicazione-piano-performance.md)
  - Which moments engage
  - Emotional arc
  - Correlation with recall, persuasion

- **UX/Website**: 
  - Eye tracking, mouse tracking, scroll depth
  - Click heat maps
  - Funnel drop-off analysis
  - Form abandonment patterns

- **Retail/In-store**: 
  - Heat map traffic patterns
  - Dwell time per zone
  - Attention tracking via eye gaze
  - Shelf visibility testing

- **Brand perception**: 
  - EEG during brand exposure
  - Implicit association test (IAT) for unconscious preferences
  - Voice tone analysis in testimonials

### 6. Motivation & Need Theories

#### 6.1 Maslow Hierarchy (classico e limite)
- **5 livelli**: physiological → safety → love/belonging → esteem → self-actualization
- **Critica**: non universale (collectivist cultures enfasi su belonging rispetto individual self-actualization)
- **Utility**: framing communication per livello
- **Modern interpretation**: livelli paralleli, non strettamente sequenziali

#### 6.2 Self-Determination Theory (Deci-Ryan)
- **3 basic psychological needs**:
  - **Autonomy**: sentirsi agenti della propria vita, scelta, controllo
  - **Competence**: sentirsi capaci, mastery, effectiveness
  - **Relatedness**: connessi agli altri, appartenenza, cura

- **Motivation types**:
  - **Intrinsic**: attività per se stessa (enjoyment, interest, alignment con values)
  - **Extrinsic**: attività per outcome esterno (reward, punishment, social approval)
  - **Amotivation**: assenza di intenzione di agire

- **Application marketing**:
  - Brand che soddisfano questi 3 bisogni ottengono engagement profondo
  - Autonomy: "personalize your experience", choice architecture
  - Competence: "progress tracking", skill-building, mastery language
  - Relatedness: "join community", shared values, insider language

#### 6.3 Jobs-to-Be-Done (Clay Christensen) — vedi Part B sezione 2

### 7. Neurobiologia della Decisione (Integrazione)

#### 7.1 Amygdala & Emotion
- Valutazione emotiva veloce, spesso subconscia
- Activazione amygdala = salience, urgency
- Base per System 1 decisions

#### 7.2 Ventromedial Prefrontal Cortex (vmPFC)
- Value integration: combina utilità razionale con valore emotivo
- Decision making conscia vs inconscia
- Reward sensitivity varies (dopamine sensitivity)

#### 7.3 Insula
- Disgust, regret, risk perception
- Activation insula durante risky choice = aversion
- Applicazione: avoid framing that triggers disgust or regret (negative visuals, loss language)

#### 7.4 Nucleus Accumbens
- Reward anticipation, dopamine release
- Motivates approach behavior
- Applicazione: anticipation messaging, milestone rewards

#### 7.5 Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (dlPFC)
- Cognitive control, working memory, deliberation
- Attivazione dlPFC durante "hard" decisions
- Applicazione: complex decisions need simplification (reduce dlPFC load)

## PART B — INNOVATION FRAMEWORKS CLASSICI

### 1. Stage-Gate Process (Robert Cooper)

#### 1.1 Il Framework
Processo lineare-iterativo per new product development con disciplined gates:

1. **Discovery/Ideation (pre-stage 0)**: trigger events, idea sources (customer, employee, competitor, technology)
   - Focus: generare idea base
   - Output: articulated opportunity

2. **Scoping (Stage 1)**: quick assessment — market, technical, business feasibility (1-3 settimane)
   - Focus: preliminary research, not deep
   - Output: preliminary business case, go/kill decision

3. **Build Business Case (Stage 2)**: detailed investigation, specification, plan (4-8 settimane)
   - Focus: comprehensive market research, technical feasibility assessment
   - Output: detailed business case (market size, competitive position, financials, resource plan)

4. **Development (Stage 3)**: design, prototype, testing (weeks to months)
   - Focus: product engineering, parallel validation
   - Output: workable prototype, test plan

5. **Testing & Validation (Stage 4)**: in-house, field, market test
   - Focus: confirm market need, product works, business case holds
   - Output: test results, decision on full launch

6. **Launch (Stage 5)**: full commercial launch + post-launch review (3-6 months post-launch)
   - Focus: sales/marketing execution, production ramp
   - Output: post-launch review, lessons learned

#### 1.2 Gates (decision points tra stage)
- **Go/Kill/Hold/Recycle decision** made by gate-keepers
- **Must-have criteria**: prerequisiti non-negotiable (strategic fit, product advantage, market attractiveness, acceptable financial risk)
- **Should-have criteria**: scored su scala 1-10, aggregated con weight, cutoff threshold
- **Gate keepers**: cross-functional senior team con budget authority
- **Evidence standard**: increases with stage (rough data in Scoping, detailed in Build Business Case)

#### 1.3 Stage-Gate Next Gen (Agile-Stage-Gate Hybrid)
- **Integration con Agile**: sprint dentro stage, iterative development
- **MVP approach**: dentro Build Business Case, small experiments to test assumptions
- **Parallel execution**: stage running in parallel (vs pure linear), especially Development + early Testing
- **Continuous customer feedback loop**: voice of customer integrated throughout
- **Flexibility**: ability to recycle back to earlier stage without kill (if new info emerges)

#### 1.4 Success Factors
- **Front-end heavy**: discovery + scoping robusti (80% di fallimenti vengono dalle cattive idea selection)
- **Customer-centric**: voice of customer in ogni stage, not internal assumptions
- **Cross-functional team**: marketing, engineering, finance, operations rappresentati
- **Disciplined portfolio management**: no sacred cows, kill projects without sentiment
- **Metrics-driven decision**: clear scoring, no politics, transparent
- **Speed**: shorten stage duration through parallel work, MVP testing
- **Organizational alignment**: executives committed to discipline

#### 1.5 Common Pitfalls
- **Weak front-end**: jumping to development without proper scoping
- **Gate criteria unclear**: gates diventano subjective, slow
- **Over-documentation**: analysis paralysis, opportunity window closes
- **Silo mentality**: marketing/engineering/finance not aligned
- **Portfolio imbalance**: all Horizon 1, no innovation pipeline
- **Stage duration creep**: stages expanding (1-2 settimane → 6-12 months)

### 2. Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD) Framework (Christensen, Ulwick)

#### 2.1 Core Concept
Esempio classico: McDonald's milkshake — commuters buy milkshake not because they love taste, but to "hire" it for the job of:
- **Functional**: staying awake, having something to do during morning commute
- **Emotional**: feeling normal, not bored
- **Social**: not relevant in this context

Key insight: demografici (30-45 uomo professionista) non predicono, ma job circumstance (40 min commute, no breakfast, alone) sì.

#### 2.2 Tipologie di Job
- **Functional job**: il compito pratico da completare (passare il tempo mentre guido)
- **Emotional job**: come voglio sentirmi (not annoyed, engaged)
- **Social job**: come voglio essere percepito dagli altri (less relevant for solitary activities)

#### 2.3 JTBD vs Demographics vs Psychographics
| Approccio | Predittività | Limitazioni |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Demographics | Bassa | Troppo broad, ignora circostanza |
| Psychographics | Media | Stable nel tempo, ma non circostanza-driven |
| Jobs | Alta | Richiede ricerca qualitativa profonda |

#### 2.4 Job Statement Formula
"When I [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]"

Es: "When I commute to work at 7am without breakfast, I want to stay alert and engaged during the drive, so I can arrive ready for my first meeting"

#### 2.5 Outcome-Driven Innovation (Tony Ulwick)
Metodologia per sistematizzare JTBD:

1. **Job mapping**: decomporre job in steps
2. **Outcome articulation**: per ogni step, quali outcomes desiderati?
   - Es. for "stay alert" job: want energy boost velocity (non intensity), want taste to not overwhelm (not strong aftertaste)
3. **Importance × Satisfaction measurement**:
   - Importance: how important is this outcome? (1-10)
   - Satisfaction: how satisfied are you with current offerings? (1-10)
4. **Opportunity score**: importance + (importance − satisfaction)
   - Formula rewards: high importance, low satisfaction = high opportunity
5. **Outcome opportunity prioritization**:
   - **High opportunity**: high importance, low satisfaction → innovation target
   - **Adequately served**: high importance, high satisfaction → parity (don't innovate)
   - **Not important**: low importance, high satisfaction → ignore
6. **Solution design**: address high-opportunity outcomes

#### 2.6 Example: Diaper Market
- **Traditional segmentation**: age of baby (newborn, 6 months, etc.) or demographic (income)
- **JTBD lens**: 
  - Job: "keep baby dry and comfortable so I can sleep" (parents job)
  - Outcomes: fast absorption (importance 8, satisfaction 3 = opportunity 13), leak protection (8/5=11), diaper change frequency low (7/4=10), rash prevention (9/6=12)
  - Innovation target: fast absorption, rash prevention (vs competitor focus on leak protection which already served)

#### 2.7 Criticalities
- **Hard to execute**: requires deep customer understanding, not just surveys
- **Job vs product confusion**: must define job, not product features
- **Context matters**: same person, different job in different context
- **Dynamic**: job evolves (baby grows, parent needs change)

### 3. Design Thinking (IDEO / Stanford d.school, Tim Brown)

#### 3.1 Le 5 Fasi (Non-Linear)
1. **Empathize**: research con utenti (interview, shadowing, immersion, observation)
   - Goal: deep understanding of user needs, pain, context
   - Not: "ask them what they want"
   - Method: ethnographic research, day-in-life, shadowing
   - Output: empathy map, user needs articulated

2. **Define**: insight → problem statement (POV — point of view)
   - Goal: articulate challenge from user perspective
   - Avoid: defining solution, defining engineering problem
   - Output: POV statement: "User [name] needs [need] because [insight]"
   - Example: "Healthcare worker needs better way to track vital signs because current system fragmented across devices"

3. **Ideate**: brainstorming divergent → convergent (How Might We questions)
   - Goal: generate many ideas, defer judgment
   - Method: brainstorming, SCAMPER, forced connections, Delphi
   - HMW questions: "How Might We help [user] [need] in [context]?"
   - Output: 100+ ideas, wild + practical

4. **Prototype**: low-fidelity rapid (paper, LEGO, storyboard, role-play, wireframe, video)
   - Goal: make idea tangible, learn through making
   - Speed: days not weeks
   - Fidelity: low to start (increases iteration friction)
   - Output: physical or digital prototype, not necessarily working

5. **Test**: user testing → iterate
   - Goal: gather user feedback, test assumptions
   - Method: observation, interview, task-based evaluation
   - Iterate: return to Empathize/Define/Ideate as needed
   - Fail fast mantra: rapid cycles of prototype-test

#### 3.2 Principi Fondanti
- **Human-centered**: start con human needs (not technology, not business constraints)
- **Collaborative**: cross-functional, cross-skill, diverse perspectives
- **Iterative**: fail fast, learn fast, cycling through phases
- **Bias toward action**: prototype early, avoid over-analysis paralysis
- **Optimistic**: assume solution exists, human-centered solution possible
- **Empathetic**: deep respect for user perspective
- **Visual/Tangible**: make ideas visible (not just talk)

#### 3.3 Tools Operativi

**User Research**:
- **Persona**: archetype utente con demographics, needs, pain points, goals, context
  - Should: behavioral not demographic (not "John, 35, male" but "John, busy professional, wants quick solutions, uses mobile")
  
- **Empathy Map**: 4 quadrants
  - **Think/Feel**: cognition, emotion, concern, preoccupation
  - **Say/Do**: what user says, actions, behavior
  - **Hear**: what they hear from others (family, friends, media, experts)
  - **See**: visual environment, market context
  - Plus: pains, gains

- **Customer Journey Map**: stages (awareness, consideration, decision, use, advocacy) × actions × emotions × pain points
  - Visual timeline of user experience
  - Identify key moments (moments of truth)
  - Discover pain points per stage

**Problem Definition**:
- **How Might We (HMW)**: reframe problem as opportunity
  - "Customer struggles to navigate website" → HMW "make navigation intuitive"
  - Not: "build new website" (solution-focused)
  - Multiple HMW from same insight

**Ideation**:
- **Brainstorming rules**: 
  - Defer judgment (no criticism)
  - Go for quantity (100 ideas)
  - Build on ideas (hitchhike, combine)
  - One conversation (no side-bars)
  - Get visual (sketch, not words)
  - Bold wild ideas (encourage wild)

- **SCAMPER**: Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse
  - Prompt creative thinking
  
- **Forced connections**: combine unrelated domains
  - "How would Netflix approach healthcare?"
  - "How would Starbucks approach office design?"

- **Dot voting**: rapid prioritization by team
  - Each person votes (dots) on best ideas
  - Quick consensus without debate

**Prototyping & Communication**:
- **Storyboarding**: visualize user experience (comic-style narrative)
  - Scene by scene, user perspective
  
- **Role-play**: act out user experience, service design
  - Stakeholders play roles
  - Reveal hidden design issues

- **Assumption mapping**: what must be true for this to work?
  - Riskiest assumptions identified
  - Test these first

#### 3.4 Differenze con Stage-Gate

| Aspetto | Stage-Gate | Design Thinking |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| Approccio | Lineare con gate, structured | Iterativo, non-linear, user-centric |
| Focus primario | Process/governance | User empathy, needs |
| Documentazione | Heavy (business case, specs) | Light, visual, iterative |
| Time horizon per fase | Months to years | Days to weeks (per phase) |
| User involvement | Late (testing stage) | Early and continuous |
| Team composition | Functional silos | Cross-functional, diverse |
| Risk mitigation | Gate discipline | MVP/prototype learning |
| Best for | Risk-heavy, regulated, mature product | Early exploration, creative problem-solving, new domains |
| Success metric | Financial ROI, time-to-market | User satisfaction, adoption |

#### 3.5 Hybrid: Design Thinking + Stage-Gate
- **Stage 0-1** (Scoping): Design Thinking for problem definition
- **Stage 2** (Business Case): Build business case around DT insights
- **Stage 3-4** (Development + Validation): Agile development with continuous user testing
- **Stage 5** (Launch): Design thinking for go-to-market strategy

### 4. Blue Ocean Strategy (Kim & Mauborgne, W. Chan Kim, Renée Mauborgne)

#### 4.1 Red Ocean vs Blue Ocean Conceptually
- **Red Ocean**: mercato esistente, competizione diretta su features/price, sangue spill da concorrenza
  - Assumption: industry attractiveness fixed, value trade-off inevitable (cost vs differentiation — Porter generic strategies)
  - Outcome: commoditization, race to bottom, marginal players exit

- **Blue Ocean**: mercato nuovo, unexplored, competizione irrilevante
  - Assumption: industry attractiveness can be created, value innovation possible (differentiation + cost leadership simultaneously)
  - Outcome: value creation, profitable growth, first-mover advantage

#### 4.2 Value Innovation (il core)
**Simultaneamente**:
- **Differentiation** (aumentare value percepito): unique features, experience, brand
- **Cost reduction** (ridurre cost structure): process efficiency, eliminate waste, different business model

**Non trade-off** (vs Porter generic strategies dove scegli cost leadership OR differentiation).

**Formula**: High price + low cost = high margin + mass market = blue ocean

#### 4.3 Strategy Canvas
Grafico che mostra:
- **Asse X**: fattori competitivi della categoria (es. per hotel: price, location, luxury amenities, restaurant, pool, room size, personalization service, etc.)
- **Asse Y**: offering level di ciascun competitor (scale 1-10)
- **Obiettivo**: creare curva distintiva vs incumbents (value innovation curve)

**Example: Southwest Airlines Strategy Canvas**:
- **Eliminate**: meals (cost), seat assignment, hub-and-spoke complexity
- **Reduce**: amenities, flight duration (vs connecting flights)
- **Raise**: frequency, turnaround speed, customer friendliness
- **Create**: direct point-to-point routes, entertainment casual experience, rapid boarding
- Result: strategy curve low cost (meals out) + high value (frequency, speed, fun)

#### 4.4 Four Actions Framework (ERRC Grid)
Systematic way to build blue ocean strategy:

- **Eliminate**: quali fattori la categoria dà per scontati (ma costavano) andrebbero eliminati?
  - Esempio: hotel chains eliminate check-in desk (self-service kiosk), telephone operator (app), expensive concierge service
  
- **Reduce**: quali fattori arebbero ridotti sotto lo standard industriale (cost saving)?
  - Esempio: hotel reduce room size, reduce housekeeping frequency, reduce restaurant options
  
- **Raise**: quali fattori arebbero aumentati sopra lo standard industriale (differentiation)?
  - Esempio: hotel raise room amenities (quality mattress, rainfall shower), raise location accessibility, raise mobile experience
  
- **Create**: quali fattori (mai offerti nella categoria) arebbero creati (new value)?
  - Esempio: hotel create themed rooms (gaming, work), create community experiences, create co-working spaces

#### 4.5 Casi Classici (Detailed)

**Cirque du Soleil (Circus → Theatrical Performance)**:
- **Traditional circus**: animals, star performers, multiple rings, slapstick humor, dangerous stunts, cheap seating
- **Cirque du Soleil**:
  - Eliminate: animals (costly, ethical, performance variability), star performers (expensive), multiple rings (confusing)
  - Reduce: storyline complexity (focus on theme), act variety (specialized not breadth), slapstick humor
  - Raise: artistic quality, original music/art, theatrical set design, thematic coherence, audience sophistication (adult-oriented)
  - Create: unique theme each show, acrobatics as art form, original IP (vs licensed), intimate venues (not huge tents)
- **Result**: Blue ocean between circus (cheap, entertainment) and theater (expensive, art) — premium circus as theatrical art

**Nintendo Wii (Console Gaming → Motion Gaming)**:
- **Traditional gaming**: HD graphics, complex controls, hardcore gamers, single player focus
- **Nintendo Wii**:
  - Eliminate: HD graphics (vs PS3), complex button controls, hardcore/male focus
  - Reduce: hardware power, game library depth (fewer titles), online multiplayer
  - Raise: ease of use (motion intuitive), family/social gaming, accessibility (non-gamers)
  - Create: motion control, family party games, living room experience, exercise games (Wii Sports, Wii Fit)
- **Result**: Blue ocean expands gaming to non-gamers (families, elderly) — gaming democratization

**Yellow Tail Wine (Wine → Approachable Wine)**:
- **Traditional wine**: complex terminology, aging focus, wine expert gatekeeping, variety overwhelming
- **Yellow Tail**:
  - Eliminate: complex wine terminology, aging story, regional complexity, variety (4000 wines)
  - Reduce: range (single grape focus), price (mass-market)
  - Raise: ease of drinking (approachable), fun naming/branding, single grape clarity
  - Create: approachable brand voice, kangaroo branding (fun, different), tasting notes for beginners
- **Result**: Blue ocean for non-wine-drinkers — wine as casual drink, not status symbol

**Southwest Airlines (Airline → Low-Cost Direct)**:
- **Traditional airline**: hub-and-spoke, meals, seat assignment, connecting flights, business focus
- **Southwest Airlines**:
  - Eliminate: hub-and-spoke (direct routes), meals, assigned seats, business first-class
  - Reduce: flight duration (point-to-point direct faster), amenities, luggage service (passengers manage own)
  - Raise: frequency (departure timing), boarding speed (rapid turnaround), customer friendliness, schedule reliability
  - Create: point-to-point network (faster), casual service culture, employees empowered (fun)
- **Result**: Blue ocean for budget-conscious travelers — airline as transportation (not service)

#### 4.6 Tre Caratteristiche di una Buona Blue Ocean Strategy
1. **Focus**: strategia focalizzata on few key factors, not tries to be everything
   - Avoid: "We offer luxury AND low cost AND service AND innovation" (unfocused)
   - Goal: clear positioning tradeoff
   
2. **Divergence**: strategy canvas diverge dal average industry
   - Avoid: me-too competitive moves
   - Goal: visual distinctiveness on canvas
   
3. **Compelling tagline**: clear value prop in una frase
   - Esempio: "Circus + Theatre" (Cirque du Soleil)
   - Esempio: "Gaming for everyone" (Wii)
   - Avoid: generic ("Great service at low price")

#### 4.7 Criticalities e Limitazioni
- **Market creation assumption**: Blue ocean assumes you CAN create new demand (not always true)
- **Timing**: early-mover advantage can flip if market doesn't materialize
- **Sustainability**: blue ocean fades as competitors copy (blue → red eventually)
- **Corporate fit**: blue ocean may require different culture, competencies vs core business
- **Portfolio risk**: blue ocean projects high risk, need portfolio approach

### 5. Innovation Portfolio Management (McKinsey Three Horizons)

#### 5.1 I 3 Orizzonti
- **Horizon 1 (Core)**: core business, improvements incrementali, 70% risorse, ROI immediato (1 anno)
  - Focus: optimize, extend profitable business
  - Examples: line extension, continuous improvement, cost reduction
  - Risk: low, success rate: 80%+
  
- **Horizon 2 (Emerging)**: emerging business, estensioni adjacent, 20% risorse, ROI 2-3 anni
  - Focus: expand into adjacent markets or business model
  - Examples: new customer segment, new channel, new product adjacent
  - Risk: medium, success rate: 60%
  
- **Horizon 3 (Transformational)**: transformational, nuove venture, 10% risorse, ROI 5+ anni
  - Focus: explore new markets, disruptive innovation, new capabilities
  - Examples: blue ocean strategy, new technology, new industry
  - Risk: high, success rate: 10-20%

#### 5.2 Innovation Ambition Matrix (Nagji-Tuff, HBR 2012)
Matrice 3x3: prodotti esistenti → nuovi × mercati esistenti → nuovi

- **Core (existing product, existing market)**: 80% of success, 10% of return
  - Examples: efficiency improvements, line extension
  
- **Adjacent (new product, existing market OR existing product, new market)**: 60% success, 20% return
  - Examples: new segment, new distribution, new application
  
- **Transformational (new product, new market)**: 10% success, 70% return
  - Examples: blue ocean, disruptive technology
  - High risk/high reward

#### 5.3 Portfolio Balancing
- **Diversificazione tra orizzonti**: don't put all eggs in core or transformational
- **Metrics diverse per orizzonte**:
  - H1 = financial ROI, time-to-market
  - H2 = market share of new category, customer adoption
  - H3 = learning rate, option value created, strategic alignment
  
- **Governance separata**: 
  - H1: traditional gate, financial scrutiny
  - H3: learning gate, strategic option value
  - Different review cadence (H1 quarterly, H3 annual with learning goals)
  
- **Resource allocation**: intentional, not emergent
  - Avoid: H1 projects consuming H3 budget (fighting for same engineers)
  - Separate teams, separate leadership, separate metrics

#### 5.4 Common Portfolio Issues
- **Portfolio creep**: all H1 (safe) no H3 (risky), pipeline dries up
- **Misallocation**: high-potential H3 starved for resources
- **Category confusion**: calling line extension "innovation"
- **Wrong governance**: applying H1 discipline to H3 (kills exploration)
- **Insufficient diversity**: all H2 no H1 or H3 (imbalanced)

### 6. Lean Startup & Rapid Experimentation (Eric Ries, Steve Blank)

#### 6.1 Build-Measure-Learn Loop
Rapid iteration cycle, fundamental to innovation speed:

1. **Build**: create MVP (Minimum Viable Product)
   - Goal: smallest experiment to test critical assumption
   - Not: beautiful, complete, but testable
   - Speed: days to weeks
   
2. **Measure**: collect data on user behavior
   - Goal: objective measurement, not vanity metrics
   - Examples: conversion rate, activation, retention, net revenue
   - Not: page views, downloads (can be inflated, actionless)
   
3. **Learn**: extract insight, validated learning
   - Goal: confirm or invalidate assumption
   - Data → decision
   - Pivot or persevere

#### 6.2 Validated Learning
- **Assumptions esplicite**: what must be true?
  - Desirability: do users want this?
  - Feasibility: can we build it?
  - Viability: can we make money?
  
- **Experiments rapidi**: test highest-risk assumption first
  - MVP size depends on assumption (can be paper prototype, landing page, fake door)
  
- **Actionable metrics** (vs vanity):
  - Actionable: directly linked to business outcome
  - Examples: activation rate (% new users who take key action), retention rate, revenue per user
  - Vanity: inflated metrics (downloads without activation, page views without engagement)
  
- **Cohort analysis**: segment users by acquisition week/version, track cohort retention
  - Reveals if improvement (v2 retention > v1), not just absolute metric

#### 6.3 Innovation Accounting (Three Stages, Ries)
Framework to measure startup progress on innovation:

- **Stage 1: Establish baseline**
  - Measure current metrics (activation, retention, revenue)
  - Create runaway (projected growth if trends continue)
  - Determine if track leads to success
  
- **Stage 2: Tune engine of growth**
  - A/B test features, messaging, pricing
  - Goal: improve metrics incrementally
  - Examples: change onboarding flow, test price point, reduce friction
  
- **Stage 3: Pivot or persevere decision**
  - If metrics improving toward target: persevere
  - If no improvement despite tuning: pivot (change direction)
  - Structured decision, not emotional

#### 6.4 Pivot Types (Ways to Change Direction)
- **Zoom-in pivot**: feature becomes product (Slack was internal tool communication)
- **Zoom-out pivot**: product becomes feature (Instagram was check-in app, pivoted to photo-sharing)
- **Customer segment pivot**: same product, different user (YouTube was video dating site)
- **Customer need pivot**: same customer, different solution (Nintendo Wii gamers wanted accessibility not power)
- **Platform pivot**: product → platform (Amazon started retail, became marketplace + AWS)
- **Business architecture pivot**: changes business model (B2B → B2C2B, subscription → freemium)
- **Value capture pivot**: monetization change (Google free search → AdWords revenue model)
- **Engine of growth pivot**: viral vs paid vs sticky model (Facebook viral, Salesforce sales-driven)
- **Channel pivot**: distribution change (direct sales → self-serve online)
- **Technology pivot**: same value, different tech (Instagram moved from Foursquare check-in to photo)

#### 6.5 Speed & Learning Rate
- **Cycle time reduces risk**: shorter Build-Measure-Learn cycles = faster learning = faster pivots before capital depleted
- **Batch size matters**: small batch (MVP simple) → faster cycle, faster learning
- - Large batch (feature-complete) → slow cycle, risk building wrong thing
- **Fail fast philosophy**: failure acceptable if learning is extracted
