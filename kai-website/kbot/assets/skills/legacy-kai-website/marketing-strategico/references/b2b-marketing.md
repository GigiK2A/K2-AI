# B2B Marketing Operativo

## 1. Specificità del Marketing B2B vs B2C

### 1.1 Differenze Strutturali

| Dimensione | B2B | B2C |
|---|---|---|
| **Numero buyer** | Pochi (oligarchia) | Molti (milioni) |
| **Dimensione transazione** | Grande (€ o $ alti) | Piccola (€ medio-bassi) |
| **Ciclo di vendita** | Lungo (6-18 mesi enterprise) | Breve (giorni/settimane) |
| **Complessità decisionale** | Molto alta (multi-step, stakeholder) | Media (decisore singolo/coppia) |
| **Razionalità vs Emotività** | Prevalentemente razionale (ROI) | Mix razionale + emotivo |
| **Numero decisori** | Molti (buying center 5-9 persone) | Uno/due |
| **Canali distribuzione** | Pochi, lunghi, integrati | Molti, brevi, distribuiti |
| **Importanza relazione** | Critica (partnership durevole) | Transazionale/episodica |
| **Ruolo prezzo** | Importante ma non prevalente (TCO) | Prevalente (price elastic) |
| **Criticità post-vendita** | Alto (implementation, support) | Basso (self-service) |
| **Personalizzazione** | Massima (custom solutions) | Standard (mass market) |
| **Budget approvazione** | Processi formali (RFP, tender, gate) | Informale (carte di credito) |

### 1.2 Derived Demand (Domanda Derivata)

Fondamento concettuale del B2B: la domanda non nasce dal bisogno diretto del buyer, ma dalla domanda che i suoi clienti manifesteranno.

**Esempio**: Un produttore di acciaio vende ai car maker perché i consumatori comprano auto. Se la domanda di auto cala del 20%, la domanda di acciaio cala >20% (bullwhip effect: amplificazione).

**Implicazioni strategiche**:
- Monitorare il mercato finale (consumer) più che quello diretto (intermediario)
- Volatilità amplificata: pianificare scorte/capacità su scenari downside
- Scenario planning: analizzare 3-5 scenari della domanda finale nei prossimi 12-24 mesi
- Partnership con clienti per visibilità: demand forecast sharing, EDI (Electronic Data Interchange)
- Diversificazione clienti: non dipendere da uno/due buyer dominant

### 1.3 Tipologie di Mercati B2B (Webster-Wind)

**1. Producer Markets (Mercati Produttivi)**
- Vendita di materie prime, componenti, semilavorati a produttori industriali
- Esempi: acciaio a car maker, componenti elettronici a telefonica, petrolio a raffinerie
- Caratteristica: derived demand, sensitivity ciclo economico, decisioni basate su cost+quality+reliability

**2. Reseller Markets (Mercati Distributivi)**
- Grossisti, distribuidori, retailer che rivendono prodotto/servizio
- Esempi: distributore farmaceutico vende a farmacie; distributore IT vende a negozi di informatica
- Caratteristica: focus su margine, servizio, supporto commerciale; loro clienti finali sono B2C o B2B
- Dinamica: controllo del margine e della visibilità della domanda (sell-through)

**3. Government Markets (Mercati Pubblici)**
- Acquisti per enti governativi, pubbliche amministrazioni, istituzioni
- Esempi: appalto per scuole, forniture ospedaliere, infrastrutture comunali
- Caratteristica: procurement rigido (bandi, gare), vincoli normativi, decision time molto lungo, buyer deterministico

**4. Institutional Markets (Mercati Istituzionali)**
- Ospedali, università, enti non-profit, associazioni, musei, organizzazioni culturali
- Esempi: catering per mensa universitaria, arredi per ospedale, licenze software per scuole
- Caratteristica: budget limitato, decision consensuale, valenza sociale, non-profit orientation

## 2. Il Buying Center (Webster-Wind, 1972)

Modello che spiega chi interviene nella decisione d'acquisto organizzativa e quale ruolo svolge.

### 2.1 I 7 Ruoli (con esempi concreti)

**1. Initiator (Iniziatore)**
- Chi riconosce il bisogno o genera la necessità di acquistare
- Esempio: responsabile della produzione che avverte che i macchinari sono invecchiati e genera RFI (Request for Information)
- Strategia vendita: creare awareness del problema, posizionarsi come problem-solver
- Touchpoint consigliato: early in the buying journey, educational content

**2. Influencer (Influenzatore/Tecnico)**
- Esperti interni che definiscono le specifiche tecniche e valutano le soluzioni alternative
- Esempio: CTO valuta quale piattaforma cloud scegliere; ingegnere di produzione valuta i parametri del macchinario
- Potenza: alta (veto power), ma raramente decisione finale
- Strategia vendita: technical spec sheet, webinar tecnico, POC (Proof of Concept), peer review
- Touchpoint consigliato: sales engineer, technical documentation, community forums

**3. Decider (Decisore)**
- Chi ha l'autorità finale di scegliere il fornitore
- Esempio: VP Operations per acquisti > €100k, CEO per M&A
- Spesso coincide con l'Approver, talvolta delegato al Buyer
- Strategia vendita: business value (ROI), risk mitigation, long-term partnership positioning
- Touchpoint consigliato: executive level, business case, references, SLA

**4. Approver (Approvatore)**
- Chi autorizza il budget e l'acquisto (quasi sempre C-level: CFO, CEO, CMO, CTO)
- Esempio: CFO approva budget >€500k; CEO approva partnership strategica
- Caratteristica: decisione su risorse, quindi rischio finanziario/reputazionale centrale
- Strategia vendita: financial ROI, payback period, cost avoidance, brand safety
- Touchpoint consigliato: board deck, risk assessment, financial model

**5. Buyer/Procurement**
- Chi negozia formalmente i termini e gestisce il contratto
- Esempio: procurement manager negozia prezzo, SLA, payment terms
- Caratteristica: leverage su prezzo/condizioni, ma raramente su strategia
- Strategia vendita: competitive pricing, flexible payment terms, efficient contracting
- Touchpoint consigliato: procurement portal, pricing transparency, eNPS

**6. Gatekeeper (Guardiano dell'accesso)**
- Chi controlla il flusso di informazioni e l'accesso ai decision maker
- Esempio: segretaria del CEO, responsabile di procurement che filtra i vendor, team di pre-valutazione
- Potenza: HIGH se ben informato; can block deal
- Strategia vendita: building relationship, clear communication, rispetto dei processi
- Touchpoint consigliato: relationship building, early engagement, respect gatekeeping role

**7. User (Utilizzatore)**
- Chi userà effettivamente il prodotto/servizio nella pratica operativa
- Esempio: commesso user del sistema POS, ingegnere che userà il software CAD
- Influenza: spesso moderata sulla decisione, ma cruciale per post-implementazione
- Strategia vendita: usability, training, support, user experience
- Touchpoint consigliato: user forum, training material, support documentation, UX testing

### 2.2 Power-Interest Matrix (Mendelow)

Strumento per analizzare stakeholder e definire strategie di engagement per ogni cluster.

```
                    HIGH INTEREST
                        |
        KEY PLAYER       |        KEEP SATISFIED
        (Initiator)      |        (C-level Approver)
        (Decider)        |        (Top customer)
                         |
     ─────────────────────────────────────── POWER
                         |
        KEEP INFORMED    |        MONITOR
        (User)           |        (External consultant)
        (Influencer)     |        (Industry analyst)
                    LOW INTEREST
```

**Strategia per quadrante**:

- **High Power + High Interest (Key Player)**: Coinvolgimento massimo (steering committee, monthly touchpoint, custom solution, executive sponsor assegnato)
- **High Power + Low Interest (Keep Satisfied)**: Comunicazione regolare ma efficiente (quarterly business review, ROI report, risk mitigation assurance)
- **Low Power + High Interest (Keep Informed)**: Aggiornamenti frequenti (newsletter, user group, webinar, feedback loop)
- **Low Power + Low Interest (Monitor)**: Minimal effort (quarterly update, standard touchpoint)

## 3. Il Processo di Acquisto Organizzativo (Robinson-Faris-Wind, 8 Step)

Modello classico (1967) che rimane valido per acquisti complessi B2B. Successivamente esteso da Johnston-Bonoma con 8 variabili di complessità.

### 3.1 Gli 8 Step del Processo

**Step 1: Problem Recognition (Riconoscimento del Problema)**

Trigger interno o esterno che genera la necessità di acquistare.

Trigger interni:
- Failure di equipment: macchina si rompe, necessità sostituzione urgente
- Depletion: stock di consumabili finisce, reordine automatico
- Performance inefficiency: costi operativi crescono, ricerca efficienza (cost reduction program)
- Scadenza contratto: revisione periodica di fornitore

Trigger esterni:
- Competitor move: concorrente introduce feature nuova, pressione a innovare
- Vendor initiative: sales rep del fornitore propone soluzione nuova
- Regolamentazione: nuovo standard normativo richiede adeguamento
- Articolo/ricerca: scopre nella stampa di industria una soluzione non nota

Strategia vendita: essere il trigger esterno → thought leadership, PoV su mercato, alert servizio (es. "il tuo contratto scade fra 6 mesi")

**Step 2: General Need Description (Descrizione Generale del Bisogno)**

Traduzione del problema in requisiti generici (non ancora specifiche tecniche).

Esempio: "Abbiamo bisogno di ridurre i costi di logistics del 15-20% nei prossimi 12 mesi" (bisogno generico) vs. "Abbiamo bisogno di un WMS (Warehouse Management System) con capacità di real-time tracking" (specifiche).

Attore: Initiator + Influencer definiscono il gap fra current state e desired state.

Documento: generalmente internal memo o kickoff meeting, non documentato formalmente.

Strategia vendita: fornire framework per definire il bisogno (e.g., "10 KPI da monitorare nella logistics"), anchor sul valore (es. "costo medio per unità trattata è X, best-in-class è Y").

**Step 3: Product Specification (Specifiche del Prodotto/Servizio)**

Dettagliamento tecnico del requisito in specifiche formali.

Documento: RFI (Request for Information) o RFQ (Request for Quotation) interno, che diventa poi base dell'RFP pubblica.

Attori: Influencer (tecnici) + Procurement scrivono le specifiche.

Contenuto tipico di una RFQ:
- Functional requirements (cosa deve fare)
- Non-functional requirements (performance, scalabilità, security, uptime, support SLA)
- Integration requirements (API, standard, data format)
- Compliance & governance (data privacy, auditing, certification)
- Timeline di implementazione

Strategia vendita: influenzare le specifiche early (technical pre-sales, architecting the spec); se la spec è già definita, provare a standard-setter come input (es. suggerire metric aggiuntiva che favorirebbe la soluzione).

**Step 4: Supplier Search (Ricerca del Fornitore)**

Identificazione della long list di potenziali vendor.

Canali di ricerca (in ordine di frequenza B2B):
1. Google search (60% dei B2B buyer inizia qui)
2. Trade directory / industry association (es. Forrester Vendor Landscape, Gartner Magic Quadrant)
3. Referral da peer / customer (word-of-mouth ancora dominante in B2B)
4. Industry conference / trade show (40% dei B2B buyer visita fiere)
5. LinkedIn (crescente: 58% dei B2B buyer ricerca vendor su LinkedIn)
6. Consultant / analyst recommendation (3rd party credibility)
7. Advertising (low funnel, dopo awareness)

Long list → short list (da 5-10 a 3-4 vendor) avviene qui; criteri: pricing range, reputation, capacity.

Strategia vendita:
- SEO/SEM per "problem + solution keywords" (es. "cost reduction logistics")
- Thought leadership (white paper, webinar, analyst coverage)
- Referral program (incentivare customers attuali a referral)
- LinkedIn outreach (trovare Initiator, posizionarsi come advisor non salesman)
- Presence in Gartner/Forrester (critical per brand awareness in Enterprise)

**Step 5: Proposal Solicitation (Invito a Presentare Proposte)**

Invio formale della RFP ai vendor selezionati.

RFP contiene: background, requirements, timeline, deliverables attesi, formato risposta, criteria di valutazione.

Attori: Procurement coordina RFP, Influencer scrive technical spec, Decider approva RFP.

Timeline: RFP → vendor response (2-4 settimane) → demo/POC (4-8 settimane) → valutazione

Deliverables attesi da vendor:
- Proposal documento (10-50 pagine)
- Demo / webinar (live o recorded)
- POC / pilot agreement (spesso gratuito per deal grandi)
- Reference customer (buyer vuole parlare con chi ha usato soluzione)

Strategia vendita:
- RFP response: pulito, allineato a criteri espliciti, con binding commerciali (price holder 30-60 giorni)
- Demo: non sales pitch, ma tailored su use case buyer
- POC: derisking (limitato scope, 30-60 giorni, KPI chiari su cosa success)
- Reference: allineare con customer simile (industry, size, maturity) che può testimonial

**Step 6: Supplier Selection (Selezione del Fornitore)**

Valutazione formale dei vendor e scelta.

Modalità di valutazione:
- **Scoring Model**: griglia con 10-20 criteri (es. capability, price, support, reference, timeline) con weight (es. capability 40%, price 30%, support 20%, timeline 10%). Punteggio 1-5 per criterio → score totale.
- **Decision matrix**: matrice criteri × vendor con ranking
- **Committee decision**: buying center vota, spesso 80% consensus required

Criteri di valutazione (esempi):
- Technical fit (60% delle volte dominante)
- Price/Commercial terms (30-40%)
- Vendor stability / size (20%)
- Support & SLA (20%)
- Implementation timeline (15%)
- Reference / case study (10%)
- Contract flexibility (10%)

Documento output: Selection board recommendation con justification.

Strategia vendita:
- Pre-scoring call: allinearsi su criteria e weight (advocacy per criteri dove sei forte)
- Demo: far leva su weakness competitor (benchmark, efficiency)
- Reference: selecting che è ready a talk in scoring timeline
- Commercial: essere primo a offrire term flessibili (sconto volume, payment plan)

**Step 7: Order-Routine Specification (Specifiche di Ordine e Condizioni Routinarie)**

Negoziazione dei termini commerciali finali: prezzo, SLA, delivery, pagamento, warranty, responsabilità.

Attori: Procurement + Approver negozia, Buyer prepara ordine/contratto formale.

Contentious points (tipici):
- Prezzo: sconto volume, payment terms (net 30/60/90)
- Delivery: timeline, milestone, penalty per delay
- SLA: uptime, response time, availability, penalties per non-compliance
- Support: hours (24/7 o business hours), response time, included vs paid
- Warranty: periodo, cosa coperto, responsabilità
- Liability cap: cap su damages (es. 12 mesi di fee)
- Data ownership: chi possiede i dati, diritto accesso, portability
- Termination: early exit clause, notice period, wind-down support

Outcome: Contract firmato, Purchase Order emesso, implementation plan avviato.

Strategia vendita:
- Contratto pre-negoziato (templates, standard terms): fa muovere il deal più veloce
- Flexibility su price ma non su value (es. sconto ok, ma non ridurre SLA)
- Commercial alignment early (non aspettare Step 7; allineare già Step 5)
- Legal review: legit vendor (not startup in stealth) → buyer fiducia

**Step 8: Performance Review (Revisione della Performance)**

Post-implementazione: valutazione del vendor vs. attese (SLA, KPI, support quality).

KPI monitorati:
- Technical: uptime, response time, # issues resolved, quality
- Commercial: on-time delivery, pricing accuracy, billing disputes risolti
- Support: CSAT, response time media, resolution time, training effectiveness
- Business: ROI realized, adoption rate (%), user satisfaction

Timeline: reviews periodiche (monthly/quarterly in primo anno, poi quarterly/annual).

Outcome: renewal decision (continue, renegotiate, exit).

Strategia vendita:
- Proactive monitoring: inviare report mensile con KPI (demoing value)
- Executive sponsor: assegnare contact person che "cares" (non solo support)
- Expansion: durante Performance Review, proporre add-on / upgrade (expansion revenue)
- Reference: chiedere permesso per fare case study / reference se performance è buona

### 3.2 Buying Situations (Robinson-Faris-Wind): 3 Tipologie

**New Task (Nuovo Acquisto)**

Primo acquisto della categoria. Buyer ha zero esperienza con il problema e la soluzione.

Caratteristiche:
- Ciclo LUNGO: 3-12 mesi (enterprise IT, manufacturing equipment)
- Molti stakeholder: buying center ampio (5-9 persone)
- Molti step: buyer percorre tutti gli 8 step
- Incertezza alta: information seeking, comparison, pilot/POC

Strategia vendita:
- Educazione: il tuo ruolo è insegnare, non vendere
- Thought leadership: white paper, webinar, case study
- Risk mitigation: reference, POC, contract con exit clause
- Sponsor executive: assegnare account executive dedicato, non junior sales

**Modified Rebuy (Revisione Contratto Esistente)**

Buyer ha esperienza con la categoria, ma sta rinegoziando il contratto attuale (scadenza) o aggiungendo capability.

Caratteristiche:
- Ciclo MEDIO: 2-6 mesi
- Buying center RIDOTTO: 3-5 persone
- Alcuni step abbreviati: skip RFI, no POC (esperienza pregressa)
- Incumbente advantage: fornitore attuale ha 60-70% probabilità di rinnovare

Strategia vendita (se incumbent):
- Account management proattivo: engagement regolare, business reviews
- Value realization: dimostrare ROI realizzato, KPI raggiunti
- Expansion: proporre feature aggiuntive, nuovi use case

Strategia vendita (se challenger):
- Competitive differentiation: focus su weakness incumbent (lentezza, cost, support)
- Risk positioning: "switch cost basso, 30-day exit clause"
- Relationship: far conoscere al buying center personas non-incumbent (es. nuovo CTO)

**Straight Rebuy (Riordino Automatico)**

Buyer ha relazione consolidata con fornitore, riordina automaticamente (consumabili, servizi routinari).

Caratteristiche:
- Ciclo BREVE: poche settimane, spesso <1 mese
- Buying center MINIMO: a volte solo Buyer (procurement), talvolta neanche coinvolto (automated reordering)
- Zero step formale: no RFP, no demo, direct order
- Switch cost ALTO per buyer (cambiare standard, retraining)

Strategia vendita (se incumbent):
- Customer satisfaction: SLA non-negotiable, support proattivo
- Lock-in positivo: integrazione profonda (es. API per automated reordering), switching cost (training specifico)
- Pricing: non aggressivo (buyer not shopping); focus su retention, volume growth

Strategia vendita (se challenger):
- Molto difficile: switch cost alto. Target: quando buyer è insoddisfatto (service failure, price shock, new buyer persona in ruolo)
- Value proposition: "Cost 20% lower with same quality" (compelling se credibile)
- Pilot: "30-day trial, risk-free switch" (lower barrier entry)

### 3.3 Differenze per Tipo di Acquisto (Johnston-Bonoma)

**Capital Equipment (CapEx, >€500k)**

Macchinari, impianti, infrastrutture IT

Caratteristiche: ciclo 6-18 mesi, molti stakeholder, CEO/CFO approval, focus su ROI/payback, maintenance/support critici

Strategia vendita:
- Build executive sponsor relationship (non sales rep)
- Financial model: TCO, payback period, NPV
- Risk: warranty, training, post-sale support contract
- Reference: talking with peer company CFO/COO

**Consumables (OpEx, <€100k tipicamente)**

Materiali ricorrenti, supply, servizi di routine

Caratteristiche: ciclo breve (few weeks to months), low visibility, procurement driven, price sensitive, reorder automatico

Strategia vendita:
- Efficienza: ordine online, consegna rapida, facile reorder
- Pricing: volume discount, subscription model, COGS reduction (less is less important)
- Service: supply chain visibility (inventory level, next delivery), customer self-service portal

**Software/SaaS (mix, €50k-€1M typically)**

Applicazioni, cloud services, digital solutions

Caratteristiche: ciclo 3-6 mesi (SaaS) a 6-12 mesi (enterprise), IT + business stakeholder, focus su integration/data, renewal gate crucial, churn risk

Strategia vendita:
- TCO selling: not just license fee, ma implementation, training, support, opportunity cost di transition
- Data/security: data ownership, encryption, compliance (GDPR, etc.)
- Integration: API, SSO, native connector (riduce implementation cost & risk)
- User adoption: training, customer success plan (high correlation con renewal)
- Expansion: start small (1 team), expand cross-org (land-and-expand)

**Professional Services (€100k-€10M+)**

Consulting, implementation, custom development, agency services

Caratteristiche: ciclo lungo (3-12 mesi), relationship-driven, scope creep comune, success basato su partnership

Strategia vendita:
- Scoping: detailed SOW (Statement of Work) con deliverables chiarissimi (scope creep killer)
- Team: assegnare senior person/partner (not junior), sponsor interno dedicato
- Governance: steering committee settimanale, status report, change order process formale
- Success KPI: definire upfront cosa è "success" (not "best effort")
- Long-term: positioning come strategic partner (non transaction vendor), upsell/cross-sell secondary phases

## 4. Account-Based Marketing (ABM)

Paradigma strategico che applica STP al livello di singolo account (o cluster di account) anzichè segmento.

### 4.1 ABM vs Demand Generation Tradizionale

| Aspetto | ABM | Demand Gen Tradizionale |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | Singolo account / piccolo cluster | Segmento ampio (1.000+ prospect) |
| **Approccio** | 1-to-1 personalization, multi-touch | 1-to-many, broadcast, few touches |
| **Buyer Personas** | Iper-specifiche (per account) | Generiche (per segmento) |
| **Content** | Tailored (account pain+context) | Generic (problem + solution class) |
| **Channels** | Orchestrated multi-channel (email, direct mail, ads, event, phone) | Primary digital (email, ads, organic, webinar) |
| **Sales Alignment** | Tight (sales ha veto su TAL, input su playbook) | Loose (sales consuma leads) |
| **Timeline** | Lungo (6-18 mesi per account) | Medio (3-6 mesi per lead) |
| **ROI** | Deal size larger, win rate higher, sales cycle shorter vs baseline | Volume play, CAC lower, conversion rate higher % |
| **Metriche** | Pipeline created, deal size lift, win rate vs competitor | MQL volume, MQL:SQL conversion, CAC, ROAS |
| **Ideal for** | Enterprise B2B, long sales cycle, high deal value | Mid-market/SMB, product-led, shorter cycle |

### 4.2 I 3 Modelli ABM (ITSMA, Demandbase)

**One-to-One ABM (Strategic ABM)**

Target: 1-10 top accounts, strategically critical per revenue/growth.

Esempi: top 10 global banks per vendor fintech; top 3 automotive OEM per supplier; top 5 telecom for network equipment

Caratteristiche:
- Dedicated playbook per account (30-50 pagine, custom content, messaging)
- Dedicated account team (salesperson + marketing person + post-sale success)
- Executive sponsorship: VP Sales + Director Marketing pair leads
- Long cycle: 6-12 mesi acquisition, 3-5 anni relationship
- High deal value: €500k-€10M+ ACV

Investimento: €100k-€500k per account/anno (team, content, events)

Playbook tipico per account:
1. Account strategy (background, org chart, win condition, competitive positioning)
2. Stakeholder mapping (7 ruoli + power-interest)
3. Messaging + value prop (tailored su pain+context)
4. Multi-channel orchestration (months 1-6: awareness via thought leadership + event, months 7-12: engagement via direct sales + POC, months 13-18: proposal + close)
5. Touchpoint calendar (weekly)
6. Competitive intelligence (monthly update)
7. Deal review cadence (bi-weekly with sales)
8. Success metrics (pipeline progress, deal velocity, competitor position)

Output: ROI/deal on 1-to-1 ABM è highest, ma small volume (1-3 deals/anno per account).

**One-to-Few ABM (ABM Lite, Cluster ABM)**

Target: 10-100 accounts, clusterizzati per industry/firmographic/use case.

Esempi: "Top 50 mid-market retailers in UK"; "Top 30 healthcare systems in Germany with >500 beds"; "Top 50 manufacturers in automotive supply chain tier-2"

Caratteristiche:
- Playbook per cluster (not per account), ma personalized execution
- Shared account team (1 AE covers 5-10 accounts, 1 marketing person per cluster)
- Cycle: 4-8 mesi acquisition
- Medium deal value: €100k-€1M ACV

Investimento: €50k-€150k per cluster/anno

Playbook per cluster:
1. Cluster definition + firmographic (size, geography, revenue range)
2. Shared messaging (problem+solution tailored a cluster; slight variation per account)
3. Cluster-level content (white paper, webinar, case study relevant to industry)
4. Multi-channel within cluster (email campaign to 20 prospects, targeted ads, direct mail to top 3 accounts in cluster, events)
5. List-building within cluster (growth strategy: add 10-20 new accounts/qtr from cluster)

Scalability: 1 marketing person can own 2-3 clusters, 1 AE can own 5-10 accounts

Output: volume moderate (10-20 deals/anno from cluster), higher velocity than One-to-One, lower than traditional demand gen.

**One-to-Many ABM (Programmatic ABM)**

Target: 100-1.000+ accounts, technology-driven, minimal manual personalization.

Estrategia: scale ABM via programmatic technology (AI, automation, intent data) anziché humans.

Esempi: "500 mid-market SaaS companies globally"; "1.000 SMB manufacturers in continental Europe"

Caratteristiche:
- Playbook codificato in piattaforma ABM (rules, logic, automation)
- Minimal sales resource (virtual AE, self-service nurture)
- Cycle: 2-4 mesi (shorter than other ABM)
- Smaller deal value: €20k-€500k ACV

Investimento: €200k-€500k platform + ops/anno, scalabile a 100+ accounts

Tecnologia core:
- ABM platform (Demandbase, 6sense, Terminus, RollWorks): intent data + website matching + campaign orchestration
- Intent data (Bombora, G2, TechTarget): firmographic + behavioral signals
- Marketing automation (Marketo, HubSpot): email, nurture, lead scoring
- Sales engagement (Outreach, SalesLoft): sequence, cadence, automation

Logic:
1. Firmographic screening: 1.000 target accounts (industry, size, geography)
2. Intent signal: which 200 accounts showing high intent (website visit, content download, analyst report read)?
3. Campaign: deliver personalized campaign (email, display ad, direct mail) to high-intent accounts
4. Scoring: behavioral + firmographic score → threshold for sales handoff (MQL)
5. Sales sequence: automated outreach (email + LinkedIn + call cadence)

Output: high volume (50-200 deals/anno from pool), lower win rate vs One-to-One, but efficient CAC.

### 4.3 ABM Tech Stack (Typical Configuration)

**Core ABM Platform**

### 4.4 ABM Playbook: Step-by-Step Blueprint

**Phase 1: Strategy (Month 1-2)**

*Step 1a: Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) Definition*

Definire chi è il cliente ideale in termini firmographic + behavioral + outcome-driven.

Inputs:
- Analisi customers attuali: chi sono i top 10% by revenue/profitability? industria, size, revenue, geography, use case, end-user
- Analisi lost deal: chi abbiamo perso? cosa li ha guidati al competitor?
- Market research: quali segmenti stanno crescendo? quale size ha growth target?

Output: ICP document
```
IDEAL CUSTOMER PROFILE (ICP)

Industry: Manufacturing (discrete), automotive supply tier-1/2
Size: €100M-€500M revenue
Geography: Continental Europe
End-user need: Production efficiency, cost reduction, supply chain visibility
Buying pattern: Capital equipment approval in 6-9 months, CFO/COO involvement, RFP-driven
Maturity: Implementing Industry 4.0, willingness to invest in tech
Budget: €300k-€1M capex for solution
```

*Step 1b: Firmographic Screening*

Identifica l'universo di account che match l'ICP.

Tools: ZoomInfo, Apollo.io, Clearbit, LinkedIn, D&B
Database size: 5.000-50.000 accounts

Filtri: industry + size + geography + (optional) technology signals (e.g., running SAP)

Output: Account universe 5.000+

*Step 1c: Segmentation + Tiering*

Classifica gli account per priorità (Tier 1/2/3) basato su:
- Revenue/budget (largest first)
- Fit to ICP (closest to profile)
- Growth potential (expand revenue)
- Strategic importance (key market, customer logo)
- Competitive threat (risk di perdita)

Matrix:
```
                    FIT TO ICP
                 LOW    MEDIUM   HIGH
                 
      HIGH    │  T3 │  T2   │  T1   │
 SIZE/BUDGET  │  T3 │  T2   │  T1   │
      LOW     │  X  │  T3   │  T2   │

T1 = Top tier (1-10 accounts, get One-to-One ABM)
T2 = Second tier (10-100 accounts, get One-to-Few ABM Lite)
T3 = Third tier (100-1.000, Programmatic ABM or nurture)
X = Not qualified, skip
```

Output: TAL (Target Account List) 50-100 T1+T2 accounts

**Phase 2: Targeting (Month 2-3)**

*Step 2a: Account Research*

Per ciascun T1 account, scopri:
- Organizational structure (org chart): CEO → CFO/COO/CTO, reporting line
- Budget ownership: chi approva capex, qui approva IT, chi approva OpEx
- Strategic priorities: cosa c'è nella 3-year plan? (earnings call, investor relations, press releases)
- Current vendors: chi usa oggi, satisfaction
- Recent changes: new CEO? CEO succession? merger/acquisition? new CFO?
- Initiatives: transformation program, digital, manufacturing 4.0?
- Signals di interest: website visit, content download, event attendance

Tools: LinkedIn, Crunchbase, 10-K filings, news, company website, cold call research, ZoomInfo

Output: Account brief (2 pages per T1 account)

*Step 2b: Stakeholder Mapping + Buy Center*

Per ciascun T1 account, mappa i 5-9 decision maker usando Webster-Wind.

Template:
```
ACCOUNT: Acme Manufacturing, Milan (€150M revenue, automotive supplier)

STAKEHOLDER MAPPING

1. INITIATOR: Production Director (Giorgio, giorgio@acme.it)
   Pain: production downtime cost €50k/day, equipment >20 years
   Influence: 7/10
   Current vendor: none (greenfield)
   Message: "Production uptime 99.9% → +€5M EBITDA/year"

2. INFLUENCER: Manufacturing Engineer (Lucia, lucia@acme.it)
   Pain: integration with legacy SAP, no real-time visibility
   Influence: 8/10 (technical veto)
   Current vendor: SAP for ERP
   Message: "SAP-native integration, 3-month implementation"

3. DECIDER: COO (Marco, marco@acme.it)
   Pain: capex ROI, payback <5 years
   Influence: 9/10
   Current vendor: works with top 3 global suppliers
   Message: "€400k capex, 18-month payback, €5M/year savings"

4. APPROVER: CFO (Francesca, francesca@acme.it)
   Pain: budget discipline, capex approval gate
   Influence: 9/10 (financial gatekeeper)
   Current vendor: requires bid from top 3 only
   Message: "TCO model: €400k year 1, €50k year 2-5 (maintenance), NPV €2M"

5. GATEKEEPER: Executive Assistant to COO (Marta, marta@acme.it)
   Pain: none (logistics role)
   Influence: 5/10 (access control)
   Current vendor: new contact, not known yet
   Message: "Support Marco → efficient meetings, clear agenda"

6. USER: Production manager (Team di 5) 
   Pain: usability, training time
   Influence: 4/10 (post-implementation critical)
   Current vendor: none yet
   Message: "Easy to use, 1-week training, 24/7 support"
```

Output: Stakeholder map + persona for messaging

*Step 2c: Competitive Positioning*

Per ciascun T1 account, identificare:
- Incumbent vendor (if any replacement): loro punti forti/deboli
- Alternative short-list: 2-3 competitor nostri
- Evaluation criteria: cosa buyer usa per scegliere (TCO, integration, brand, speed)
- Competitive positioning: dove siamo strong? dove déboli? (SWOT vs competition)
- Win strategy: qual è il path per vincere questo deal? cosa è critico?

Output: Competitive brief (1 page)

**Phase 3: Messaging (Month 3-4)**

*Step 3a: Account-Specific Value Proposition*

Sintesi il messaggio chiave per account, tailored su pain + context.

Non è: generic product pitch. 
È: "So what" answer per gli stakeholder, quantificato.

Template:

```

Why us: "API-first architecture, 50+ pre-built SAP connectors, zero custom dev"
Proof: "Integrated in 8 weeks at similar tier-1 supplier, zero disruption"

FOR FINANCIAL (CFO Francesca):

Why us: "Fixed price, SLA penalty clause if uptime <99%, no surprise costs"

```

### 4.5 ABM Metrics & KPI

**Engagement Metrics** (leading, monitoring account momentum)

- Account visit count: # distinct visits to website from account IP (target: >5/month for engaged accounts)
- Content engagement: # content pieces consumed (download, view, read) per account (target: >3/month)
- Email engagement: open rate (target >30%), click rate (target >5%), response rate (target >1%)
- Sales touches: # calls, meetings, emails from AE to account (target: 8-12 per month for active accounts)
- Account stage progression: time from Pre-engagement → Evaluation (target: <2 months)

**Pipeline Metrics** (lagging, outcome-oriented)

- Pipeline contribution: % of total pipeline attributable to ABM accounts (target: 40-60% of total)
- Avg. deal size (ACV): ABM accounts ACV vs. non-ABM (ABM target: 2-3x larger)
- Deal velocity: average days from first engagement to SLA (ABM target: <180 days for One-to-One, <120 days for One-to-Few)
- Win rate: % of opportunities closed-won vs. total opportunities from ABM accounts (ABM target: 30-40%, vs. 15-20% non-ABM)
- Competitive win: % of ABM deals where we beat specific competitor (monitor quarterly)

**Efficiency Metrics**

- CAC ABM: marketing cost (team + tools + content) / ABM deals closed (should be higher $ value, but lower CAC %)
- Sales productivity: revenue per AE (ABM account AE should have higher revenue/quota vs. non-ABM AE)
- Time to revenue: days from account entry to close (ABM target: 180-240 days for enterprise)
- Cost per opportunity: marketing cost per opportunity created from ABM (should be lower than demand gen)

**LTV & Expansion Metrics**

- Upsell/cross-sell: $ from expansion revenue in year 1 post-sale (ABM target: 20-30% of ACV by month 12)
- NRR (Net Revenue Retention): (ARR end of period - churn + expansion) / ARR start of period (target: >110% for healthy ABM account)
- Churn rate: % of ABM customers lost to competitor / no-renewal (target: <5% annually)
- CLTV: Lifetime value of ABM customer account (should be 3-5x higher than non-ABM due to expansion + retention)

## 5. Sales Qualification Frameworks

Quando una lead emerge nel funnel, usare framework per determinare se è qualificata per handoff a sales (MQL → SQL) e quale è lo "readiness" della deal.

### 5.1 BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline)

Classico framework IBM, semplice ma limitato.

**B – Budget**: Qual è il budget disponibile per questa categoria di spesa?
- Domanda: "Qual è il vostro budget approvato per questa soluzione?"
- Red flag: "Non abbiamo ancora approvato budget" (non qualificato)
- Green flag: "€500k riservati nel capex 2026" (qualificato, timeline chiaro)

**A – Authority**: Chi ha l'autorità di decidere/approvare l'acquisto?
- Domanda: "Chi dovrà approvare la decisione di acquisto?"
- Red flag: Risponde qualcuno che non è approver ("Io devo chiedere al CFO"), no clear champion
- Green flag: "Io (CFO) approvo, consultando COO" (clear authority path)

**N – Need**: C'è un bisogno specifico/riconosciuto?
- Domanda: "Qual è il problema che vi spinge a cercare una soluzione?"
- Red flag: Vague ("Dovremmo forse cercare una soluzione"), no pain quantificato
- Green flag: "Abbiamo downtime media 8 ore/mese, cost €50k/evento, urgenza alta" (pain quantificato)

**T – Timeline**: Qual è il timeline di decisione/acquisto?
- Domanda: "Quando prevedete di fare la scelta e firmare il contratto?"
- Red flag: "Non sappiamo ancora" or "Forse anno prossimo" (no urgency)
- Green flag: "Vogliamo RFP a gennaio, decision marzo, implementazione maggio" (clear timeline)

**Scoring BANT**:
- 4/4 criteri ✓ = SQL (Sales Qualified Lead), ready for proposal
- 3/4 criteri ✓ = MQL, nurture 1-2 month then re-qualify
- <3 criteri ✓ = disqualify or long nurture (6+ month)

**Limiti**:
- Non considera competitor activity o switching cost
- Non mapping buying center (BANT è per single person, not committee)
- Non considera technical fit o implementation complexity

### 5.2 MEDDIC / MEDDPICC (Enterprise Deal Qualification)

Framework più robusto, usato per deal complessi (€100k+).

**M – Metrics**: Quali metriche di business si impattano?
- Domanda: "Se implementiamo questa soluzione, quali metriche miglioreranno?"
- Ricerca: quantificare il delta (production downtime -50%, cost -€2M/anno, time-to-market -6 mesi)
- Outcome: assicurare che buyer ha riconosciuto l'impatto economico
- Red flag: Buyer non sa rispondere, no quantificazione

**E – Economic Buyer**: Chi ha l'autorità economica (budget)?
- Non è il "decision maker" generico, è specificamente chi controlla i soldi
- Example: CFO approva €500k capex, quindi CFO è economic buyer (non COO even if COO è sponsor)
- Domanda: "Chi ha il diritto di dire sì al budget di €500k?"
- Red flag: Talking a manager, economic buyer non identificato; manager dice "devo chiedere al CFO"

**D – Decision Criteria**: Quali criteri l'azienda usa per scegliere?
- Domanda: "Quali sono i criteri che userete per valutare i vendor?" (poi: "Voi come li peserete?")
- Esempio: Capability 40%, Price 30%, Support 20%, Speed implementazione 10%
- Ricerca: assicurare che criteri sono noti e che noi siamo strong nei criteri weighted più alto
- Azione: se criterio importante è "open-source", competitor vede vantaggio; noi diam di focus su integration, not open-source posture

**D – Decision Process**: Quali step il buyer seguirà per decidere?
- Domanda: "Qual è il vostro processo di decision? Quanti step? Chi involved? Quali gate?"
- Esempio: 1) RFI (Jan), 2) RFP (Feb), 3) Demo (Mar), 4) POC (Apr-May), 5) Evaluation (Jun), 6) SLA negotiation (Jul), 7) Executive approval (Aug), 8) Signature (Sep)
- Ricerca: map ogni step, identify decision gate, identify who's involved, timeline per step
- Azione: align sales/marketing plan con buyer's process (not our process, their process)
- Red flag: "Process non è ancora definito" (riski di scope creep, delay)

**P – Paper Process** (or **Paperwork**, or **Purchase Process**)
- Domanda: "Quali approvazioni, documenti, processi legali/compliance sono necessari per finalizzare l'acquisto?"
- Ricerca: identify non-negotiable proces (RFQ banding, legal review, compliance check, board approval se >€1M)
- Esempio: "Serve approvazione board (riunione q.li), legal review (2-3 weeks), compliance check su data privacy (1-2 weeks)"
- Azione: anticipare questi step nella timeline (se non precedentemente considerati, timeline è underestimated)
- Azione: prepare contract template che passi legal review quickly (non surprises)
- Red flag: "Non siamo sicuri" on paper process; organization non mature su procurement

**I – Identify Pain**: Qual è il pain specifico, quantificato, urgente?
- Domanda: "Qual è il costo specifico del non agire?" oppure "Se il problema persiste, cosa vi costano?"
- Ricerca: identificare pain che non è vago ("efficienza") ma quantificato ("€2M/anno")
- Azione: harping on pain in messaging (es. in email: "downtime di 8 ore = €400k loss per voi")
- Ricerca: assicurare pain è urgent (not "nice-to-have", ma "must-have")
- Red flag: "È un progetto nice-to-have" (non qualificato come SQL, stay in nurture)

**C – Champion**: Chi internamente spinge per noi?
- Champion ≠ contact (contact è la persona che parla con noi, champion è la persona che parla con organnizzazione interna)
- Domanda: "Chi all'interno supporterà la nostra soluzione con altri stakeholder?"
- Ricerca: identificare chi è vested interest (chi beneficia da soluzione, o chi il boss ha detto di find solution)
- Azione: giving champion evidence to win internally: email template, talking points, business case per loro boss
- Azione: coach champion on competitive objections
- Red flag: "Nessuno internamente supporterà" (deal is high-risk per implementation)

**C – Competition**: Chi altro è nel deal? (Competitor analysis)
- Domanda: "Quali alternative state considerando?" oppure "Quali fornitori siete in discussione?"
- Ricerca: identify named competitors, understand loro position (è incumbent? è newcomer?)
- Ricerca: understand loro strengths vs. nos (es. "incumbent has integration advantage, noi abbiamo speed advantage")
- Azione: develop competitive narrative (es. "vs. incumbent: 3-month faster, vs. newcomer: proven with peers")
- Azione: attack competitor weakness in proposal (es. se competitor slow, brag nostra speed)
- Red flag: "Non ci sono competitor, farai qualsiasi cosa tu voglia" (unlikely; forse buyer nasconde)

**MEDDPICC Extended** (added by Winning by Design):
- Identificare la **Process** (already covered in decision process)
- Identificare il **Pain** (already covered)

### 5.3 SPICED (Winning by Design framework, newer)

Alternative più snella, focus su momentum + key insights:

**S – Situation**: Qual è la situazione attuale del prospect?
- Domanda: Industry, size, current solution, maturity level
- Output: background brief

**P – Pain**: Qual è il pain punto?
- Domanda: "Cosa non funziona oggi? Qual è il costo?"
- Output: quantified pain statement

**I – Impact**: Se risolvessimo il pain, qual è l'impact commerciale?
- Domanda: "Se risolviamo il problema, quali metriche / revenue / cost migliorano?"
- Output: business case, quantified

**C – Critical Event**: Qual è l'evento che rende urgente la decisione adesso?
- Domanda: "Perché stanno cercando una soluzione oggi, non il mese prossimo?"
- Trigger: scadenza contratto, nuova budget approvazione, competitor move, regulatory change
- Output: timeline trigger

**E – Economic Buyer**: Chi approva budget?
- Same as MEDDIC

**D – Decision**: Qual è il processo di decision?
- Same as MEDDIC decision process + decision criteria

**Vantaggi SPICED**: snello (6 element, vs 8 in MEDDPICC), focus su critical event (urgency), più memorizabile

### 5.4 Quale Framework per quale Situazione?

| Deal Size | Cycle Length | Buyer Maturity | Recommended Framework |
|---|---|---|---|
| <€50k | <3 months | Transactional | BANT (suffica) |
| €50k-€500k | 3-6 months | Mid-market | BANT + SPICED |
| €500k-€5M | 6-12 months | Enterprise | MEDDIC |
| >€5M | 12+ months | Strategic | MEDDPICC + Stakeholder mapping |

**Practice**: Combina framework (es. BANT as initial screen, poi MEDDIC deeper dive per SQL).

## 6. Demand Generation vs Lead Generation

### 6.1 Definizioni

**Demand Generation**: Creare consapevolezza e interesse nella categoria/brand/soluzione, alimentando il top del funnel con prospetti qualificati (ma non necessariamente pronti a comprare adesso).

Obiettivi:
- Awareness: il prospect non sa che problema ha, o conosce categoria ma non brand
- Education: il prospect sa che ha un problema, capisce le opzioni
- Trust: il prospect considera noi come credibile fonte di insight (expert positioning)
- Timeline: lungo (6-24 mesi nurturing before sale)

Metrics: brand awareness (unaided %), content consumption, website traffic, email list growth, social followers, podcast listeners

**Lead Generation**: Catturare contatti qualificati pronti a entrare il sales funnel (MQL/SQL stage).

Obiettivi:
- Conversion: far sì che il prospect dia il contatto (email, phone)
- Qualification: assicurare contact è buyer potenziale (right person, right company, right need)
- Handoff: deliver leads a sales con data ≤5 min (response SLA critical)
- Timeline: breve (conversion in settimane/mesi, handoff in giorni)

Metrics: lead volume, lead quality (MQL to SQL %), response time, CAC, cost per lead, lead-to-opportunity conversion

### 6.2 Demand Generation Tactics (Moderno, Dark Funnel Focus)

"Dark funnel": il 98% dei buyer journey è invisibile (non trackable). Demand gen lives in dark funnel.

**1. Content Marketing "Dark" (No Gating)**

Produce high-value content che non chiede email/registrazione, distribuisci liberamente.

Formati:
- LinkedIn long-form posts (expert insights, 300-1000 word, on topic di industria/market/strategy)
- Podcast (20-45 min episodes, CEO/founder as host/guest, distribuzione su Spotify/Apple)
- YouTube channel (educational, how-to, not salesy, 5-15 min per episode)
- Blog posts (SEO-optimized, ungated, 1500-3000 word, how-to or framework)
- Twitter/X threads (insights, tips, banter)
- Email newsletter (ungated, valuable insights, no ask for a long time)

Intent: build trust + establish expertise (brand asset) → eventually convert to lead later when they're ready

**2. Brand Awareness Campaigns**

Run paid campaigns (LinkedIn ads, Google display, YouTube TrueView) with goal awareness not lead capture.

KPI: reach, frequency, brand lift (survey metric: "consider us" pre/post campaign)

### 6.3 Lead Generation Tactics

**1. Gated Content (High Conversion)**

Require email to access premium content.

Conversion: 5-25% (high utility = high conversion)

Direct conversion tactic for SaaS.

Demo request: "Schedule a 30-min call with our solutions engineer" → calendly link or form → sales inbound

Free trial: "Try for 30 days free, no credit card" → sign-up → self-service onboarding → sales check-in at day 7 → nurture if no-engagement or upgrade

Conversion: demo request 2-5% (high intent), trial signup 10-20% (lower barrier)

In-person (trade show, user conference, webinar event) + virtual (online summit).

- Lead capture form at booth (trade show): scan badge, capture data

Conversion: referral-driven lead = 25-40% conversion to SQL (highest quality source vs. other channels)

### 6.4 B2B Funnel: MQL → SQL → Opportunity → Closed Won

**Funnel architecture (typical B2B SaaS):**

```
Visitor (100%)
   ↓ 1-3% conversion
MQL (Marketing Qualified Lead)
   ↓ 25-35% conversion
SQL (Sales Qualified Lead)
   ↓ 50-60% conversion to Opportunity
Opportunity (deal stage, $)
   ↓ 20-30% conversion
Closed Won
```

**Define transitions**:

MQL → SQL: When?
- Explicit: lead fills out "request a demo" form + matches ICP
- Implicit: lead engagement score >50 points (visited 5 pages + downloaded 2 pieces of content + clicked email 3x), lead is CTO or VP Eng at company >100 people
- Manual: sales rep reviews and assigns as "sales accepted lead" (SAL)

SQL → Opportunity: When?
- Explicit: sales completes qualification call, confirms BANT/MEDDIC 4/4, enters opportunity in SFDC
- Implicit: created in SFDC with €10k+ ACV and probability >10%

Opportunity → Closed Won: When?
- Contract signed + payment received (or commitment binding)

**Benchmark conversion by stage** (varies by industry/product):

| Stage | Conversion Rate | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Visitor → MQL | 1-3% | very top funnel |
| MQL → SQL | 25-35% | sales qualification, can be high if scored well |
| SQL → Opportunity | 50-60% | opportunity creation is often manual, lower rate |
| Opportunity → Closed Won | 20-30% | very deal-dependent (deal size, competition, cycles) |

**Leading indicator (leading by 30-60 days)**: MQL volume growth → SQL volume growth (30-day lag) → Opportunity growth (60-day lag) → Revenue (90-day lag)

## 8. Sales-Marketing Alignment

### 8.1 SLA (Service Level Agreement) Marketing-Sales

Formal agreement signed by VP Marketing + VP Sales defining reciprocal commitments.

**Marketing Commitments**:

- Lead volume: X MQL per month (e.g., 500 MQL/month)
- Lead quality: lead scoring threshold for MQL (e.g., engagement score >50 OR fits ICP firmographic + downloaded content)
- Lead diversity: % lead from each channel (e.g., 40% demand gen, 30% partner, 30% event)
- Timeliness: lead notification within 2 hours of form submission (hot lead SLA)
- Data quality: lead data 90% complete (name, company, email, title)
- Feedback loop: monthly meeting with sales to get disqualification feedback; incorporate into scoring model

**Sales Commitments**:

- Response time: call lead within 5 minutes (hot lead) or 24 hours (regular lead)
- Disqualification reporting: 100% of disqualified leads receive reason code (budget, timeline, fit, competitor, no-priority)
- CRM discipline: every lead logged in SFDC within 24h of contact, call notes added, disposition set
- Feedback loop: monthly SLA review meeting with marketing, share top objections + feedback

**Shared Metrics** (tracked weekly/monthly):

- MQL volume (actual vs. plan)
- MQL-to-SQL conversion rate (target >25%)
- SQL-to-opportunity conversion (target >50%)
- Average sales cycle (target <180 days for SMB, <360 days for enterprise)
- Win rate (target 20-30%)
- Pipeline contribution (% of pipeline from marketing-influenced sources, target 40-60%)
- Revenue attribution (% of closed-won revenue traceable to marketing campaign)

### 8.2 Lead Handoff Process

**Step 1: MQL Definition + Scoring**

Marketing defines what makes a lead "marketing qualified" via combination of:

- Explicit signals (high-intent actions):
  - Form submission with "request demo" CTA
  - Webinar registration + attendance
  - Content download (gated whitepaper, ebook, case study)
  - Free trial sign-up
- Implicit signals (engagement scoring):
  - Engagement score (email open +1, click +2, website visit +3, content download +5, demo request +10)
  - Lead scoring model: B(behavior) + F(firmographic) = score. MQL threshold = score >50
  - Firmographic filters: company size >10 employees, company revenue >€1M, industry match, geography match

Example MQL definition: "Lead who submitted 'request a demo' form + company is in target list of 1.000 companies + annual revenue >€100M" OR "Lead with engagement score >75 (watched 3 videos + downloaded 2 pieces of content + clicked email 5x + visited pricing page)"

**Step 2: Automatic Assignment Rules**

Once MQL criterion met, automatically route to sales (CRM + email notification):

- Territory assignment: route by company geography/region (e.g., Milan leads → Milan sales team)
- Account assignment: if account is known (in SFDC), route to assigned AE
- Round-robin: if new account, distribute evenly among available reps

Automation: HubSpot workflows or Salesforce flow

**Step 3: Response Time SLA (5-Minute Rule Critical)**

First response time is critical: <5 min response = 100x higher likelihood of qualification vs. >30 min response

Tactic:
- Sales alert (SMS + Slack + email) when lead arrives
- Dedicated "lead response" person on schedule (one person each hour takes lead call)
- Workflow: lead arrives 10:04 AM → SMS alert + Slack alert to assigned AE → AE calls by 10:09 AM → documents result in SFDC

**Step 4: Initial Qualification Call**

AE calls lead within 5 min (while lead is warm):

- Goals: confirm buyer fit (company, title, budget), identify stage (early-stage or later), surface pain, get next step
- Duration: 5-10 min (quick conversation, not full pitch)
- Framework: opening intro + 1 question on situation + 1 question on timeline + address immediate objection + next step CTA ("Can I send you X?" or "Can I schedule deeper call next week?")
- CRM entry: within 5 min of call end, log company, contact title, next step, pain summary, forecast stage
- Disposition: SAL (Sales Accepted Lead, qualified) or disqualified (reason code)

**Step 5: Feedback Loop (Monthly + Quarterly)**

- Weekly: brief sync on lead quality (any surprises? consistent scoring?)
- Monthly: detailed review (volume, MQL→SQL %, disqualification reasons, objections)
  - Action: identify patterns (e.g., "leads from LinkedIn have lower SQL%, let's adjust")
  - Action: update scoring model based on feedback (e.g., "remove 'visited blog post' from MQL criteria, only count demo request + firmographic")
- Quarterly: SLA review meeting (full P&L on lead channel, ROI per channel, renewal SLA or updates)

### 8.3 Revenue Operations (RevOps): L'Evoluzione

RevOps emerged in 2015-2020 as evolution of silos (Sales Ops + Marketing Ops + Customer Success Ops).

**What it is**: Integrated operational function reporting to Chief Revenue Officer, responsible for:

- Process: standardize and optimize selling process (pipeline definition, stage gates, deal review cadence, territory planning)
- Technology: own go-to-market tech stack (CRM, MA, sales engagement, customer success, BI tools), integration, data quality
- Data: ensure clean data (lead dedupe, account reconciliation, revenue attribution), analytics, reporting
- Enablement: train sales, marketing, CS teams on process + tools

**Org structure** (typical):

```
Chief Revenue Officer
├─ VP Sales (AEs, sales leadership)
├─ VP Marketing (demand gen, content, campaigns)
├─ VP Customer Success (implementation, onboarding, account management, renewal)
├─ VP Revenue Operations
   ├─ Sales Operations Manager
   ├─ Marketing Operations Manager
   ├─ Data Analyst / BI
   ├─ Systems Administrator (CRM + integrations)
```

**Key responsibilities of RevOps**:

1. **Pipeline management**: define stages, criteria for stage movement, forecast accuracy, quarterly planning
2. **Quota + territory**: annual territory planning, quota setting, alignment with forecast, fairness review
3. **Lead routing**: assignment logic, capacity planning (AE workload), territory coverage
4. **SLA enforcement**: monitor marketing SLA (lead volume, quality), sales SLA (response time, CRM discipline)
5. **Data hygiene**: deduplication (1 company = 1 account record), contact consolidation, data validation rules
6. **Tech stack**: CRM strategy (Salesforce vs. HubSpot, customizations), integration (Zapier, API), security
7. **Reporting**: pipeline dashboard, forecast accuracy, win/loss analysis, revenue attribution
8. **Process design**: design sales process, gate criteria, approval workflows, deal review cadence
9. **Training + enablement**: onboard new hires on process, tools, systems

## 9. B2B Pricing Specifico

### 9.1 Enterprise Pricing Models

**1. Subscription-based (SaaS standard)**

Price per user/month or per account/month, often with annual discount.

Example: "€99/user/month or €1.188/user/year (20% discount for annual)"

Variants:
- Per-seat: price × # of users
- Per-feature: tiered features (Starter €99/user, Professional €199/user, Enterprise custom)
- Per-usage: pay per transaction/API call (less common for SaaS, common for cloud infrastructure)

Advantage: predictable revenue (MRR), align price with value (more users = more value), expansion revenue (add users over time)

Disadvantage: switching cost low (customer can leave each month)

**2. Perpetual License + Maintenance (Software on-premise, declining)**

One-time license fee, plus annual maintenance (15-20% of license fee).

Example: "€500k license fee + €100k/year maintenance (years 2-5)"

Typical contract: 5-year perpetual license, 5-year maintenance included, then negotiate maintenance post-5-year

Advantage: large upfront revenue, longer contract value (5-year), high switching cost (perpetual license sunk cost)

Disadvantage: declining model (fewer customers buying perpetual), customer may delay payment (depreciation schedule), large upfront support investment

**3. Consumption-based (Cloud/usage)**

Price per unit of consumption (API calls, data processed, storage, compute hours).

Examples:
- AWS: €0.0001 per API call + €0.10 per GB storage
- Snowflake: €2-4 per "credit" (compute + storage unit)
- Twilio: €0.0075 per SMS sent

Advantage: customer pays for value (low-risk entry, no minimum), align incentives (more usage = more revenue, more value)

Disadvantage: unpredictable revenue for customer (budget risk), unpredictable revenue for vendor (volatility), requires strong product (customer won't limit usage to control cost)

**4. Value-based / Outcome-based**

Price based on outcome delivered, not input consumed.

Example: "We save you €500k/year in logistics costs. You pay us 20% of savings = €100k/year"

Also called:
- Shared savings: vendor + customer split savings
- Risk-sharing: vendor only gets paid if target metric met (e.g., "30% cost reduction or price reduction applies")

Advantage: maximum alignment (vendor incentivized to maximize customer value), large deal size possible (% of customer savings can be 6-7 figures)

Disadvantage: complex pricing (hard to predict revenue), requires trust + outcome tracking, audit needed to prove savings

**5. Hybrid (Most common in enterprise)**

Combination of above models.

Example: "€2.000/month base subscription (SaaS) + €1,000 per implementation project + 15% of cost savings from optimization"

Rationale: base fee covers costs (predictability), variable fee aligns with usage/value (expansion), shared savings aligns incentives (strategic partnership)

### 9.2 Deal Desk + Pricing Governance

Enterprise deals rarely list price. Instead, deal team negotiates custom pricing based on deal size, customer importance, competition.

**Discount Approval Matrix** (typical):

| Discount Size | Approver | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| List price (0%) | Anyone (AE, rep) | Standard |
| -5% to -10% | Sales Manager | Normal competitive pressure |
| -10% to -20% | Director of Sales | Significant discount, territory important |
| -20% to -30% | VP Sales | Very large deal or strategic customer |
| >-30% | CEO + CFO | Strategic decision (loss leader, market entry) |

**Floor Price Enforcement**:

- Gross margin floor: all deals must have ≥40% gross margin (internal cost + COGS + support cost)
- If deal size large enough (€10M+ ACV), may accept 35% margin (large deal exception)
- CAC payback floor: customer LTV must be >3x CAC (if SaaS), or customer can't achieve <3-year payback (if enterprise software)

**Deal Review Cadence**:

- Weekly forecast call: all active deals >€50k reviewed, probability assessed, blockers identified
- Monthly business review: pipeline coverage (3-4x quota for healthy quota attainment), win rate trend, competitive losses analyzed
- Quarterly planning: forecast accuracy review (were we right 90 days ago?), quota adjustment for year
- Deal desk review (monthly): discounts >10% reviewed for compliance, margin floor enforced

### 9.3 Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Selling

Sell on TCO vs. license fee (purchase price). TCO includes all costs of buying + implementing + operating solution.

**TCO Framework**:

```
Year 1:
├─ Acquisition cost
│  ├─ Software license/subscription: €500k
│  ├─ Professional services (implementation): €200k
│  └─ Training + change mgmt: €50k
├─ Operational cost (Year 1)
│  ├─ Support/maintenance: €100k (20% of license)
│  └─ Infrastructure: €50k
└─ Total Year 1 TCO: €900k

Years 2-3:
├─ Operational cost
│  ├─ Support/maintenance: €100k
│  └─ Infrastructure: €50k
└─ Total Year 2-3 TCO: €150k/year = €300k for 2 years

Total 3-Year TCO: €900k + €300k = €1.200k (€400k/year)
```

**TCO vs. Status Quo**:

Customer's current logistics cost: €5M/year

Alternative A (manual, current state): 
- Cost: €5M/year
- Hidden costs: 2% of staff (€200k) handling manual, 5% error rate (€250k cost of errors)
- Total: €5.45M/year × 3 years = €16.35M

Alternative B (our solution):
- Implementation: €500k (year 1)
- Licensing: €500k/year
- Support: €100k/year (assume cost reduction: 1% of staff = €100k)
- Operational: €50k/year
- Reduced error (2% error): €100k savings/year
- Total: €1.15M year 1, €650k years 2-3 = €1.15M + €1.3M = €2.45M × 3 years

**Comparison**:
- Status quo: €16.35M
- Our solution: €2.45M
- **Savings: €13.9M (85% TCO reduction)**
- **Payback period: 5 months** (€500k implementation ÷ (€5.45M - €1.15M) monthly savings)
- **ROI: 2,700%** (€13.9M savings ÷ €500k investment)

**Selling TCO**:

1. **Educate**: send TCO framework / whitepaper early ("Total Cost of Ownership: Why Purchase Price Isn't the Full Picture")
2. **Interview**: understand customer's current costs (ask for budget data, # FTE, error rate)
3. **Model**: build TCO model specific to customer (use their data, not benchmark)
4. **Quantify**: calculate payback period + NPV + IRR (financial metrics CFO cares about)
5. **Validate**: share model, ask customer to validate inputs (accuracy critical, customer must believe numbers)
6. **Negotiate**: if customer wants lower price, trade for longer contract (lower price, but multi-year contract to recover)

## 10. B2B Metrics Dashboard

### 10.1 Leading Indicators (Predict future revenue, 30-90 day lag)

- **MQL volume**: # marketing-qualified leads per month (target: align with pipeline goal / sales team size / sales cycle)
- **SAL** (Sales Accepted Lead): # of MQLs converted to SQL by sales (target: >25% MQL→SQL)
- **Pipeline velocity**: average days from opportunity creation to close (target: shorter is better, benchmark 90-180 days SMB, 180-360 days enterprise)
- **Deal stage conversion**: % of opportunities moving to next stage per week (target: >10% weekly progression)
- **Account engagement score**: firmographic + behavioral score for ABM accounts (target: >50 for high-engagement accounts, correlates to future revenue)

### 10.2 Lagging Indicators (Outcome metrics, reported monthly/quarterly)

**Pipeline Coverage Ratio**: 
- Definition: Total pipeline $ ÷ Annual quota
- Target: 3-4x (€300k annual quota → €900k-€1.2M pipeline)
- Rationale: need 3-4x coverage to account for loss rate (70-75% of deals are lost), achieve quota

**Win Rate**:
- Definition: # closed-won deals ÷ total opportunities (%)
- Target: 20-30% (varies by industry, competitors, sales competency)
- Lower win rate (15%) → need more pipeline to hit quota, or improve sales quality
- Higher win rate (35%+) → under-serving opportunity (should take more deals, lower price, expand into adjacent market)

**Average Contract Value (ACV)**:
- Definition: total ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) ÷ # of customers acquired
- Target: ACV growth >10% YoY (expansion revenue + selling to larger customers)
- Tracking: ACV by segment (SMB ACV €20k, mid-market ACV €100k, enterprise ACV €500k+)

**Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)**:
- Definition: Sales + Marketing spend / # of new customers acquired
- Example: €1M marketing spend + €2M sales spend = €3M S&M ÷ 100 customers = €30k CAC
- Target: CAC <50% of ACV (so ACV €100k → CAC <€50k, leaving 50% gross margin for support + overhead)

**CAC Payback Period**:
- Definition: # of months to recoup CAC from customer margin
- Formula: CAC ÷ (ACV × gross margin %) ÷ 12 months
- Example: €30k CAC ÷ (€100k ACV × 70% margin) ÷ 12 = 2.1 months
- Target: <12-18 months SaaS (shorter = faster unit economics, cash flow positive faster)

**Net Revenue Retention (NRR)**:
- Definition: (ARR end of period - churn + expansion) / ARR start of period
- Formula: NRR = (€100M starting ARR - €10M churn + €15M expansion) / €100M = 105% NRR
- Target: >110% (best-in-class), >100% (healthy), <100% (declining)
- Implication: >110% NRR means customer base growing organically (expansion > churn), can fund growth from operations

**Gross Revenue Retention (GRR)**:
- Definition: (ARR end of period - churn) / ARR start of period
- Formula: GRR = (€100M - €10M churn) / €100M = 90% GRR
- Target: >90% (target minimum to be healthy), >95% (strong)
- Implication: GRR tells retention without expansion (raw customer stickiness)

### 10.3 Sales Efficiency Metrics (Strategic KPI, tracked quarterly)

**Magic Number (SaaS specific)**:
- Definition: (ARR new - ARR churn) / Sales & Marketing spend (previous quarter)
- Example: (€25M new - €5M churn) / €10M S&M spend = 2.0 magic number
- Interpretation:
  - >1.0 = efficient (€1 S&M spend generates €1 incremental ARR)
  - >1.5 = very efficient (suggest to invest MORE in S&M, can scale profitable)
  - <0.75 = inefficient (re-examine product-market fit, pricing, sales process)
- Action: if magic number declining YoY, investigate why (sales team productivity? higher CAC? lower ACV?)

**Rule of 40 (SaaS + enterprise software)**:
- Definition: Growth rate (%) + Profit margin (%) ≥ 40%
- Example: Company growing 25% revenue YoY + 15% operating margin = 40 (healthy)
- Example: Company growing 50% but -10% margin = 40 (aggressive growth, breaking even or losing $ to scale)
- Implication: >40 = healthy balance growth + profitability; <40 = re-prioritize

**LTV:CAC Ratio**:
- Definition: Lifetime Value ÷ Customer Acquisition Cost
- Formula: LTV = ACV × gross margin % × customer lifetime (years); CAC = S&M spend / customers
- Example: ACV €100k, 70% gross margin, 5-year lifetime = €350k LTV; CAC €30k → LTV:CAC = 11.7:1
- Target: >3:1 (minimum healthy), >5:1 (strong)
- Below 3:1 = unsustainable (customer doesn't generate enough profit to cover acquisition)
- Much higher (10:1) = underinvesting in growth (could spend more on sales/marketing, still profitable)

**CAC Payback (already mentioned)**:
- 12-18 months = SaaS healthy
- <12 months = efficient
- >24 months = problematic, customer LTV questionable

## Appendix: B2B Marketing Terminology

- **BDR** (Business Development Representative): sales role, <1 year tenure, prospecting + cold outreach
- **AE** (Account Executive): sales role, 1-3 years tenure, closing deals + account management
- **SAE** (Senior Account Executive): sales role, 3+ years tenure, complex deals, strategic accounts
- **SME** (Subject Matter Expert): technical person advising sales (not sales person)
- **CSM** (Customer Success Manager): post-sales, onboarding, expansion, retention
- **Pod**: small sales team (AE + BDR + SDR + CSM) reporting to manager
- **ICP** (Ideal Customer Profile): firmographic definition of best customer
- **TAL** (Target Account List): list of accounts to target with ABM
- **MQL** (Marketing Qualified Lead): lead that meets marketing qualification criteria
- **SAL** (Sales Accepted Lead): MQL that sales agreed is qualified
- **SQL** (Sales Qualified Lead): lead that sales engaged, qualified with BANT/MEDDIC
- **Opp** (Opportunity): deal in pipeline with stage + amount + close date
- **Pipeline coverage**: total pipeline $ / annual quota (target 3-4x)
- **ARR** (Annual Recurring Revenue): SaaS revenue metric, subscription revenue × contract years
- **MRR** (Monthly Recurring Revenue): ARR ÷ 12
- **NRR** (Net Revenue Retention): retention + expansion (indicator of health)
- **Churn**: % of customers lost / revenue lost (target <5% annually SaaS)
- **Logo churn**: # of customers lost (vs. $ churn)
- **Expansion revenue**: upsell + cross-sell to existing customers
- **Logo retention**: # customers retained / # customers at start of period
- **Dollar retention**: $ revenue retained (after churn - expansion)
- **Land-and-expand**: acquire SMB customer at low price, expand to full org later
- **Outbound**: proactive prospecting (cold call, email, LinkedIn)
- **Inbound**: demand-gen + marketing-sourced leads
- **Velocity**: speed of deal progression (days to close)
