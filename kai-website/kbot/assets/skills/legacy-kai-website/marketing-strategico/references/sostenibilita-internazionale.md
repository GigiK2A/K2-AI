# Sostenibilità & International Marketing

## PART A — SOSTENIBILITÀ E PURPOSE MARKETING

### 1. Framework ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance)

#### 1.1 Le 3 Dimensioni
- **Environmental**: emissioni carbonio, water usage, waste, biodiversità, energy efficiency, packaging circolare
- **Social**: human rights supply chain, D&I, employee wellbeing, community relations, product safety
- **Governance**: board diversity, executive comp, tax transparency, anti-corruption, ownership structure

#### 1.2 ESG Standards & Reporting Frameworks
- **GRI** (Global Reporting Initiative): standard più diffuso globalmente, pubblicato dal 2000. Approccio modularizzato: GRI 2 (governance), GRI 3 (materiality), GRI 200/300/400 (economic/environmental/social topics). Utilizzato da 10,000+ organizzazioni worldwide.
- **SASB** (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board): industry-specific materiality. 77 standard per settore (healthcare, technology, energy, consumer goods, financials, etc.). Focus su ciò che conta finanziariamente per investor.
- **TCFD** (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures): framework per disclosure rischio climate verso investitori. Pilastri: governance, strategy, risk management, metrics & targets. Adottato dai principali asset owner globali.
- **CSRD** (Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive, EU 2024+): obbligatorio per large EU companies (>250 dipendenti, €50M revenue/25M total assets). Double materiality concept. Sanzioni fino a 5% fatturato.
- **ESRS** (European Sustainability Reporting Standards): dettaglio tecnico implementazione CSRD. E1-E5 (environmental), S1-S4 (social), G1-G2 (governance). Pubblicato EFRAG 2023.
- **B-Corp Certification**: assessment 200-point per aziende purpose-driven. Misura impact positivo su ambiente e società, non solo profit. >7,000 B-Corp globalmente (Patagonia, Ben & Jerry's, Warby Parker).
- **UN SDG**: 17 Sustainable Development Goals come framing narrativo. Goal 1-5 (povertà, salute, educazione, genere, acqua), 6-10 (energia, lavoro, innovazione, disuguaglianza), 11-17 (città, consumo, clima, ecosistemi, pace, partnership). Spesso usato come ESG narrative framework per stakeholder engagement.

#### 1.3 Double Materiality (CSRD pillar)
- **Financial materiality**: issue che impattano financial performance dell'azienda (rischi fisici, di transizione, reputazionali)
- **Impact materiality**: impatto dell'azienda su persone, ambiente, società (diritti umani, inquinamento, community displacement)
- **Matrice 2x2**: prioritizza issue su entrambi gli assi (Alto impact + Alto financial = massima priorità)
- Obbligo CSRD di disclosure su tutti gli issue materiali, con quantificazione dove possibile
- Differenza vs SASB (solo financial) e GRI (solo impact)

### 2. Greenwashing: Identificazione e Prevenzione

#### 2.1 I 7 Sins di Greenwashing (TerraChoice 2010, updated 2024)
1. **Hidden trade-off**: claim green su un aspetto, ignora impatto negativo su altri (es. "sustainable packaging" ma inquinamento produttivo massivo)
2. **No proof**: claim senza certificazione credibile o documentazione (es. "eco-friendly" senza label riconosciuta)
3. **Vagueness**: generic "eco-friendly", "natural", "green" senza definizione specifica
4. **Irrelevance**: CFC-free su prodotti dove CFC già banned per legge (false progress claim)
5. **Lesser of two evils**: verde in categoria intrinsecamente dannosa (es. "greenest cigarettes")
6. **Fibbing**: claim apertamente falso (es. certificazione fasulla, % riciclato falso)
7. **Worshiping false labels**: certificazioni inventate che sembrano ufficiali (fake eco-labels)

#### 2.2 Green Claims Directive (EU 2024)
- Divieto claim generici "eco", "green", "sostenibile" senza prove specifiche
- Obbligatorietà back-up documentale per ogni claim
- Sanzioni fino a 4% fatturato (applicabile anche a non-EU sellers selling in EU)
- Certificazioni riconosciute (EU Ecolabel, Nordic Swan, FSC, Fair Trade) obbligatorie se claims fatto
- Ex-ante verification procedure per environmental claim novel
- Disclosure di trade-off chiaramente (es. se prodotto green ma packaging no)

#### 2.3 Checklist Anti-Greenwashing
- Claim specifici con numeri, percentuali, scope chiaramente delimitati
- Certificazione third-party credibile (non auto-certificazione)
- Scope trasparente (prodotto vs azienda vs supply chain)
- Lifecycle assessment pubblico o verifiable (ISO 14040/44 standard)
- Trend e target quantificati con baseline chiara (es. "ridotto CO2 del 15% vs 2020 baseline")
- Internal audit pre-comunicazione su accuratezza claim
- Comparabilità con competitor (non claim di "maggior green" senza benchmark)
- Aggiornamento claims regolare (non claim statico su achievement vecchio)

### 3. Purpose-Driven Marketing (complementare a brand-management-advanced.md)

#### 3.1 Simon Sinek's Golden Circle applicato a Purpose
- **WHY** (purpose, reason for being): perché l'azienda esiste oltre al profitto
- **HOW** (process, values, beliefs): come l'azienda opera, principi di azione
- **WHAT** (products, services): cosa l'azienda produce
- Branding parte dal WHY, non dal WHAT. Consumatori comprano il WHY, non il prodotto
- Esempio: Apple WHY = "innovazione e human-centered design", HOW = "minimalism, simplicity", WHAT = "computer, phone, tablet"

#### 3.2 Purpose-Brand-Product Fit Test
- Coerenza purpose ↔ brand promise ↔ product reality
- Rischio when disallineato: percezione di purpose-washing, boycott, reputational damage
- Test: consumatore sa perché il brand esiste? Prodotto allineato a quel why? Azioni aziendali coerenti?
- Esempio negativo: brand che clama sostenibilità ma fornitori worker abused → mismatch social impact

#### 3.3 Cause-Related Marketing (CRM) vs Purpose Marketing
- **Cause-related marketing (CRM)**: partnership specifica e temporale con causa sociale (tactical, campaign-based, es. "buy product, donate to charity")
- **Purpose marketing**: integrazione profonda nel DNA aziendale (strategic, long-term, parte della mission/vision)
- CRM più facile, scalabile, ma rischio di opportunism perception
- Purpose più credibile ma richiede commitment genuino, coerenza comportamentale, investimento long-term
- Esempi CRM: Pink Ribbon campaigns (Breast Cancer), Red (AIDS fight), 1% for the Planet (Patagonia model)
- Esempi Purpose: Danone "mission-driven", Unilever Sustainable Living Plan, TOMS One-for-One model (integrated in business model)

#### 3.4 Consumer Research on Purpose (Edelman, WGSN, Havas)
- **64% consumatori globali** self-identify come "belief-driven buyer" (Edelman 2023)
- **Gen Z/Millennials**: purpose 3x più importante rispetto a Boomer nella purchase decision
- **Purpose premium**: willingness to pay +5-15% per brand purpose-driven autentici (varia per categoria: beauty +12%, apparel +8%, tech +5%)
- **Purpose risk**: boycott altrettanto rapido (24-48h viral) se percepito fake o hypocrite
- **Trust correlation**: brand con clear, communicated purpose = +25% customer loyalty, +18% retention
- Geographic variance: purpose-driven stronger in North America/Northern Europe, growing in APAC/LatAm

### 4. Circular Economy e Regenerative Business

#### 4.1 Circular Economy Principi (Ellen MacArthur Foundation)
1. **Design out waste and pollution**: pensare rifiuto come input di design, non scarto (cradle-to-cradle design)
2. **Keep products and materials in use**: maximizzare product lifecycle via riparabilità, upgradabilità, modularità, resale
3. **Regenerate natural systems**: uso risorse rinnovabili, restorative agriculture, ecosystem restoration

#### 4.2 9R Framework (Reike et al., Potting et al.)
Hierarchy per order di sustainability:
- **Refuse, Rethink, Reduce**: evitare consumo
- **Reuse, Repair, Refurbish, Remanufacture, Repurpose**: estendere product life
- **Recycle, Recover**: recuperare materiali (ultimo step)

#### 4.3 Business Models Circolari
- **Product-as-a-Service (PaaS)**: subscription/lease di utilizzo invece di ownership. Cliente paga per servizio, produttore mantiene asset. Incentivi mutui per durabilità e efficienza. Esempi: Philips pay-per-lux (lighting), Rolls-Royce power-by-the-hour (engine maintenance), Michelin tire rental
- **Take-back programs**: reverse logistics per remanufacturing. Patagonia Worn Wear (resale + repair), Nike Grind (shoes → new products), Apple trade-in (upgrade cycle), H&M garment collection
- **Resale marketplaces**: secondary market platforms. Vinted, ThredUp, Back Market, Vestiaire Collective. Estendono product lifecycle, creano value capture per consumer e platform
- **Rental/Subscription fashion**: short-term access without ownership. Rent the Runway, Oltre, Grailed. Riduce fast-fashion overproduction, migliora asset utilization
- **Industrial symbiosis**: waste di uno = raw material input di altro. Kalundborg Denmark (refinery, power plant, biotech condividono steam, waste). Eco-industrial parks

#### 4.4 Regenerative Business (post-sustainability evolution)
- Non solo "less bad" (sustainability) ma "net positive" (regeneration)
- **Restorative supply chain**: agricoltura rigenerativa (Danone ricostruisce soil health), carbon-negative farming (Patagonia partner farms)
- **Ecosystem contribution**: azienda contribuisce restore ecosistema non solo evitare damage
- **Carbon negative**: Microsoft climate commitment (negative by 2030), Patagonia carbon neutral + offset
- Implicazioni: pricing premium, brand differentiation, investor appeal (ESG funds >$40T AUM)

### 5. Sustainable Marketing Mix

#### 5.1 Sustainable Product (4P)
- **Eco-design principles**: ridurre material usage, scegliere low-impact materials, design for disassembly, modular, upgradeable
- **Eco-label strategy**: EU Ecolabel (flower), Nordic Swan (Scandinavia), FSC (forestry), Fair Trade, B-Corp, Cradle-to-Cradle, Marine Stewardship Council (MSC)
- **Transparency supply chain**: blockchain traceability (ex: Everledger diamonds, Fishcoin supply tracking), third-party audit supply chain
- **Durability over disposability**: long-lasting design, repairability score (France mandatory depuis 2021), lifetime warranty model

#### 5.2 Sustainable Pricing (4P)
- **True cost pricing**: incorpora externalities (carbon cost, water depletion, social impact). Raro in pratica; più teorico/advocacy
- **Premium pricing sustainable lines**: green premium su mainstream product (es. organic +20-40% premium). Funziona se consumer perceive value aggiunto
- **Subscription over ownership**: riduce per-unit material via utilization optimization (PaaS model above)
- **Rischio**: green premium excludes mass market, diventa luxury positioning, limita scale. Opportunity: scalare sustainable base product per tutti, premium per eco-conscious

#### 5.3 Sustainable Distribution (4P)
- **Local sourcing, km zero**: riduce shipping emissions, supporta local economy, supply chain transparency. Trade-off: seasonal availability, limited variety
- **Last-mile emissions reduction**: EV delivery vehicles, cargo bike urban delivery, consolidation centers, dark stores. Amazon Climate Pledge Friendly, DHL carbon-neutral delivery
- **Green packaging**: post-consumer recycled (PCR) content, compostable/recyclable materials, refillable/reusable systems (Loop partnership Unilever/TerraCycle), minimal packaging
- **Reverse logistics infrastructure**: take-back systems, repair hubs, remanufacturing facilities. Capital intensive, ROI long-term

#### 5.4 Sustainable Communication (4P)
- **Evidence-based claims**: specifici, numerici, third-party verified (anti-greenwashing)
- **Storytelling impatto reale**: comunicare supply chain, craftspeople, environmental outcome, non solo claim
- **Accessibility report sostenibilità**: non solo investor-focused reports; consumer-friendly sustainability dashboard (goals, progress, challenges transparently)
- **Engagement con NGO per credibility**: partnership advocacy organizations (WWF, Greenpeace, Fair Trade) per third-party validation, avoided accusations di purpose-washing

### 6. Business Case di Sostenibilità

#### 6.1 Valore Finanziario (CFO perspective)
- **Revenue streams**: accesso a segmenti conscious consumer, premium pricing power, new markets (green bond financing), supplier partnerships
- **Cost reduction**: efficienza energetica (utility bill cut), waste reduction (material cost down), supply chain resilience (avoiding price volatility commodities)
- **Risk mitigation**: compliance risk (CSRD, CBAM), physical climate risk (supply chain disruption), transition risk (stranded assets, brand risk), litigation risk (shareholder derivative)
- **Access to capital**: green bonds, sustainability-linked loans, ESG funds (>$40T AUM 2024), investor activism pressure (pension funds, asset managers)
- **Talent cost**: attraction/retention cost reduction (78% dipendenti vogliono lavorare per purpose-driven company), reduced turnover, improved productivity

#### 6.2 KPI Sostenibilità (GRI/SASB/CSRD standard)
- **Environmental KPI**: Scope 1/2/3 emissions (GHG Protocol), water intensity (L/unit), waste to landfill (%), recycled content (%), renewable energy (%), biodiversity impact (hectare)
- **Social KPI**: gender pay gap (%), board diversity (%), supplier diversity (%), employee engagement score, injury rate (TRIR), community spending ($)
- **Governance KPI**: board independence (%), executive comp-performance link, tax transparency (cash tax rate), ethics violations (count), data breach (count)
- **Integrated KPI**: ESG rating (MSCI, Sustainalytics, S&P Global), B-Corp impact score, Net Promoter Score (NPS), customer satisfaction (CSAT)

## PART B — INTERNATIONAL / GLOBAL MARKETING

### 7. Standardizzazione vs Adattamento (il dibattito fondamentale)

#### 7.1 Gli Approcci Teorici (Levitt vs Douglas-Wind)
- **Levitt 1983 "Globalization of Markets"**: convergenza cultural verso homogeneous global consumer → global standardization di prodotto, pricing, promotion ottimale (efficienza scale). Limiti: ignora cultural differences, local regulation, purchasing power variance
- **Douglas-Wind 1987 "Counter argument"**: limiti globalizzazione, importanza adattamento locale su culture, regolazione, infrastruttura → market-specific strategy
- **Contingency view** (moderato): dipende da prodotto category, market maturity, consumer segment, competitive landscape
- **Empirical evidence**: luxury goods piu standardizzate (LVMH global brand), FMCG piu adattate (Nestlé localization), tech medio (Apple global brand + local variants)

#### 7.2 Drivers di Standardization
- **Scale economies**: produzione a volumi alti = cost per unit basso. Global platform production economies vs custom per market
- **Consistent brand image globale**: riconoscibilità, brand equity transfer across market, avoid brand dilution
- **Technology-driven convergence**: internet, mobile, streaming abbassa cultural barriers, omnichannel consistency requirement
- **Integrated communication**: campagna globale, asset sharing, media efficiency
- **Global consumers**: expat, business travelers, digital natives expect global brands to be consistent

#### 7.3 Drivers di Adaptation
- **Differenze culturali**: linguistic nuance, religious/ethnic sensitivities, value system (Hofstede), communication style prefer
- **Differenze normative**: advertising regulation (comparative ads banned EU), food safety, data privacy (GDPR), IP rules
- **Differenze infrastruttura**: cold chain availability (ice cream distribution Africa diverse), retail format (e-commerce penetration), digital payment ecosystem
- **Purchasing power**: affordability variance, pack size optimization (sachet India), promotional strategy
- **Linguistica e semanti**: translation challenge, color symbolism, number superstitions, image appropriateness

#### 7.4 Matrice 4P x Standardization/Adaptation (degree range)
Per ciascuna P, grado tipico di standardization:
- **Product**: core formula often standard, features/variants adattate (Coca-Cola sugar recipe regional, packaging size, flavors)
- **Pricing**: heavily adapted (income elasticity, competitor pricing, channel margin, VAT variance)
- **Place/Distribution**: adapted (retail structure varies, e-commerce penetration, logistics cost)
- **Promotion/Communication**: heavily adapted (cultural resonance, media availability, regulatory constraint, language)

### 8. CAGE Framework (Pankaj Ghemawat, Professor IESE)

Analisi della distanza tra mercati su 4 dimensioni — serve per screening, prioritization, adaptation profondità

#### 8.1 Cultural Distance (C)
- **Language**: lingua nativa, proximity linguistica (es. portoghese-spagnolo vicini vs english-mandarin lontani)
- **Religion**: religious practice, dietary restriction, holiday calendar, attitude towards materialism
- **Ethnicity**: etnia, clan system, tribal identity, immigration history
- **Social norms**: gender roles, family structure, attitude verso hierarchy, saving vs consumption
- **Values** (Hofstede dimensions, covered below): individualism, power distance, uncertainty avoidance, long-term orientation

#### 8.2 Administrative/Political Distance (A)
- **Colonial ties**: shared colonial history = lingua, legal system, trade preference
- **Trade agreements/blocs**: EU (tariff-free), NAFTA/USMCA, ASEAN, Mercosur → cost advantage, regulatory harmonization
- **Currency**: currency risk, convertibility, inflation, exchange rate volatility
- **Political instability**: corruption, government change, regulatory unpredictability, conflict risk
- **Legal system**: civil law vs common law, contract enforcement, IP protection, labor law divergence

#### 8.3 Geographic Distance (G)
- **Physical distance**: shipping cost, transport time, supply chain complexity (far markets = higher cost/risk)
- **Time zones**: same-day communication challenge, coordination difficulty
- **Climate**: product adaptation (seasonal variation), supply chain disruption (weather)
- **Common borders**: adjacency advantage (NAFTA, EU internal market), border management (tariff, customs)
- **Transportation/communication links**: infrastructure quality, digital connectivity, logistics network density

#### 8.4 Economic Distance (E)
- **Income level** (GDP per capita): affordability, package size, channel strategy (premium vs value)
- **Income distribution**: GINI coefficient, middle-class penetration, wealthy segment size
- **Cost of labor**: wage difference, manufacturing location advantage, service labor cost (sales, distribution)
- **Infrastructure**: electricity reliability, cold chain, road network, port access
- **Human capital**: education level, skill availability, tech adoption rate, digital literacy

#### 8.5 Uso Operativo di CAGE
- **Screening mercati potenziali**: calcolare distanza CAGE di target market vs headquarter. Minor CAGE = attractive entry candidate
- **Prioritization**: sequencing geopolitica (north America → Western Europe → developed APAC → emerging markets)
- **Adaptation depth**: maggior distanza CAGE = piu adattamento prodotto, pricing, communication required
- **Sequencing espansione**: espandere da vicini (low CAGE) a lontani (high CAGE), build capabilities progressively

### 9. Hofstede Cultural Dimensions (applicate al marketing)

#### 9.1 Le 6 Dimensioni (Geert Hofstede, decades di research)
1. **Power Distance Index (PDI)**: livello di accettazione disuguaglianza gerarchica. Alto = gerarchy view as normal, leadership centralized. Basso = egalitarian, decentralized
2. **Individualism vs Collectivism (IDV)**: "I" vs "We" orientation. Alto individualism = personal achievement, independence, unique identity valued. Basso = group harmony, family/community priority
3. **Masculinity vs Femininity (MAS)**: achievement orientation vs care/quality-of-life. Masculino = performance, ambition, competitiveness, status valued. Feminino = cooperation, care, work-life balance
4. **Uncertainty Avoidance (UAI)**: tolleranza ambiguità, preferenza per structured vs unstructured. Alto = structured, rules, risk-averse, tradition. Basso = flexible, novelty-seeking, risk-tolerant
5. **Long-term vs Short-term Orientation (LTO)**: long-term planning, persistence vs tradition, short-term gratification. LTO alto = future planning, savings, adaptation valued. Basso = tradition, immediate gratification
6. **Indulgence vs Restraint (IND)**: gratification di desire vs self-control. Indulgence = enjoy life, personal gratification. Restraint = self-discipline, duty

#### 9.2 Implicazioni Marketing (profile per cluster)
- **Alto PDI** (India 77, Russia 93, MENA 55-80, Mexico 81): 
  - Luxury cues, status symbols imagery
  - Hierarchical representation (leadership portrait, authority figures)
  - Brand prestige communication
  - Distribution through established channel (premium retailers, authorized distributors)
  - Pricing premium acceptable

- **Alto Individualism** (USA 91, UK 89, Netherlands 80, Australia 90):
  - Achievement narrative, personal success stories
  - Independence, uniqueness emphasize
  - Personal benefit highlight over community good
  - Direct communication style
  - Entrepreneurship celebration

- **Alto Collectivism** (China 20, Korea 18, Guatemala 6, Ecuador 8):
  - Family, community, group harmony narratives
  - Belonging, group acceptance message
  - Indirect communication, relationship-building
  - Community benefit emphasis
  - In-group loyalty reward

- **Alto Masculinity** (Japan 95, Germany 66, Hong Kong 57, Italy 70):
  - Performance, technical specifications emphasize
  - Achievement, winning frame
  - Status symbol highlight
  - Competitive advantage messaging
  - Price-quality trade-off (higher price = better quality assurance)

- **Alto Femininity** (Sweden 5, Norway 8, Denmark 16, Costa Rica 21):
  - Care, relationships, quality-of-life emphasize
  - Sustainability, wellbeing narratives
  - Work-life balance messaging
  - Cooperation, partnership approach
  - Equal treatment, fairness value

- **Alto UAI** (Greece 100, Portugal 104, Spain 86, Mexico 82):
  - Guarantees, warranties, risk reduction emphasis
  - Testimonials, proof, track record
  - Stability, proven results communication
  - Regulation compliance highlight
  - Expert endorsement value

- **Basso UAI** (Singapore 8, Denmark 23, Jamaica 13, Hong Kong 29):
  - Novelty, innovation, experimentation emphasize
  - Risk-taking, adventure narratives
  - First-mover, trendsetting messaging
  - Flexibility, adaptation value
  - Unstructured, organic brand communication

### 10. Modalità di Ingresso (Entry Modes)

#### 10.1 Spectrum Controllo vs Investimento (increasing commitment)
Spectrum from low to high control/investment:
- **Indirect Export**: tramite export agent, trading company. Controllano i termini, no market insight diretto. Low investment, low risk, low control
- **Direct Export**: azienda direttamente export, controlla termini export. Medium control, low capital
- **Licensing/Franchising**: concede diritti IP a licenziatario locale. Licensing (mfg diritti), Franchising (business format + IP). Low capital per franchisor, control limitato, revenue royalty-based
- **Joint Venture (JV)**: partnership 50-50 o other ratio con local partner. Shared capital, shared control, shared profit. Medium-high commitment
- **Wholly Owned Subsidiary** (Greenfield o Acquisition): 100% ownership. Greenfield = construisci da zero (slow, capital intensive, learn gradual). Acquisition = buy existing player (fast, high capital, instant market presence, integration risk)

#### 10.2 Fattori di Scelta (decision tree)
- **Market attractiveness** (size, growth rate, competition): large market + high growth = prefer control (JV/subsidiary). Small niche = Licensing/Export
- **Resources available** (capital, talent, brand equity): strong capital + talented team = greenfield/acquisition. Limited resources = licensing/export
- **Risk tolerance** (political, economic, operational): high political risk = low commitment (export/licensing). Stable market = willing to invest
- **Learning objectives**: if goal is learn market = JV to build relationships. If goal maximize profit = wholly-owned subsidiary
- **Speed requirement**: fast entry = acquisition. Measured entry = greenfield. Slow = licensing/export
- **Regulatory constraints**: some sectors mandate JV (automotive China pre-2020), some restrict FDI (media, telecom regulated). Constraint = licensing/JV forced

### 11. Global Brand Management

#### 11.1 Global Brand Equity (Aaker framework applicato globally)
- **Consistency**: brand name, logo, packaging, tagline, visual identity consistency across market (immediate recognition)
- **Local relevance**: execution localized (product features, pricing, communication language/cultural adaptation)
- **Iconic global brand + local sub-brands**: master brand global (Nestlé, Unilever) + local sub-brands (KitKat global + regional flavors)
- **Managing brand portfolios cross-market**: portfolio strategy (premium/mid/value tiers), avoid cannibalization, market segmentation clarity

#### 11.2 Brand Name Decisions
- **Same name globally**: Coca-Cola, McDonald's, Nike, Apple → brand equity transfer. Risk: name inappropriate in some cultures
- **Translated**: Vauxhall = Opel (UK market trademarked), Xiaomi (Chinese → English)
- **Local brands under global owner**: P&G Mr. Clean vs Mr. Propre (FR) vs Mastro Lindo (IT) vs Meister Proper (DE). Local names carry stronger equity, avoid translation awkwardness
- **Phonetic issues**: Mitsubishi Pajero → Montero in Spanish-speaking (Pajero = slang negative), Chevrolet Nova → flop in Spanish ("no va" = doesn't go)
- **Registration strategy**: reserve trademark globally before launch, monitor squatting

#### 11.3 Colore, Simboli, Numeri (cultural sensitivity)
- **Colori**: 
  - Red = luck, prosperity, celebration (China) vs danger, warning (West). Red envelope tradition China, red door feng shui
  - White = death, mourning (East Asia) vs purity, cleanliness (West). Bridal white in West, death shroud in East
  - Yellow = royalty, prosperity (China) vs caution (West)
  - Purple = luxury, magic (West) vs death/mourning (some traditions)
  - Green = prosperity, nature, health (mostly universal) but halal/Islamic context
  - Black = mourning (mostly), evil (West) vs luxury (formal wear)

- **Numeri**:
  - 4 = sfortuna (Cina/Giappone, suona come morte) → avoid product quantities, floor numbers, pricing
  - 8 = fortuna (Cina, suona come prosperità) → premium, auspicious
  - 7 = auspicious (alcuni cultura occidentali/cristiane)
  - 13 = sfortuna (Ovest, Last Supper, Friday 13). Edifici saltano floor 13
  - Significance varies by region; research required

- **Simboli animali**:
  - Cow = sacred (India Hindu) → no beef reference, no cow imagery in food context
  - Pig = problematico (paesi islamici, Judaism) → no pig imagery, avoid pork product marketing in MENA/Pakistan
  - Eagle = patriotic (USA) → safe
  - Dragon = prosperity, power (China) vs evil (Western tradition)
  - Owl = wisdom (West) vs bad omen (India)

### 12. Glocal Strategy

#### 12.1 Il Concetto (Roland Robertson 1995)
"Think global, act local" — framework balance tra global consistency (brand equity, efficiency, scale) e local relevance (cultural resonance, regulatory compliance, competitive fit)

#### 12.2 Glocal Templates
- **Global platform + local activation**: global campaign concept/theme (Nike "Just Do It", Coca-Cola "Taste the Feeling") pero execution locale (local celebrities, language, cultural moments, media mix)
- **Modular creative**: asset modulari (photography, video, layout templates) assemblati per mercato. Centralized creative production + local customization
- **Global pool + local customization**: piattaforma centrale con template/guidelines, hub regionali (EMEA, APAC, Americas) adattano per cluster geografica
- **Glocal product**: core product standard (Coca-Cola formula, iPhone hardware) + local variants (flavor regional, color preference, pricing, pack size)

#### 12.3 Organization for Glocal Execution
- **Matrix organization**: product line (global) × geographic region × function. Complex, slow, political. Apple: product (iPhone global) × region (EMEA, APAC) × function (marketing, sales, operations)
- **Center of Excellence**: specializzazione per capability/brand. One country leads innovation (es. Switzerland = luxury, Scandinavia = sustainability, India = mobile-first), distribute best practice globalmente
- **Regional hub model**: EMEA HQ (Europe-based), APAC HQ (Asia-based), Americas HQ (US-based). Hub coordina regional strategy, local country execute tactically
- **Local market empowerment**: country/market leader ha P&L responsibility, autonomy to adapt within central guardrails (brand identity, sustainability commitment, pricing bounds). Trust + accountability

#### 12.4 Glocal Metrics
- Brand health tracking (awareness, perception, preference) globally consistent pero country-granular
- Campaign performance indexed to regional KPI (market-specific conversion rate, CAC, ROAS)
- NPS/satisfaction country-level variance tracking

### 13. Emerging Markets Specifiche

#### 13.1 BoP (Bottom of Pyramid) Marketing (Prahalad 2004)
- **Market size**: 4B people <$4/day, vast untapped market. Prahalad thesis: profitability opportunity, not charity
- **Micro-packaging revolution**: single-use sachets instead of bulk (shampoo 1 dose, laundry detergent sachet, instant noodles single bowl). Affordability, trial, inventory management for retailer
- **Micro-pricing**: small pack = small price (ex. ₹5 sachet shampoo vs ₹50 bottle). Perceived affordability
- **Micro-financing**: payment plans, group lending, mobile money enabling purchase. MVNO phone credit-based purchase
- **Distribuzione capillare**: "last-mile" retail (village shops, kiosks, street vendors) instead of modern retail. Distribution density critical
- **Durability/simplicity**: ruggedized product, service layer critical (ex. Nokia 3310, motorcycle taxi service)

#### 13.2 Mobile-First Markets (India, Africa, Southeast Asia, LatAm)
- **Leapfrog**: skipped desktop era → mobile is primary/only computing device
- **Mobile money**: M-Pesa Kenya (payments, transfers), MoMo Africa, AliPay/WeChat China → digital economy without bank account
- **WhatsApp primacy**: messaging, business, customer service, marketplace integration. WhatsApp Business, WhatsApp API for CRM
- **USSD/SMS**: pre-smartphone era still relevant, basic feature phones widespread, SMS-based payment/authentication
- **Platform economy strong**: ride-sharing (Uber, Grab), food delivery (Zomato, Grab Food), e-commerce (Shopee), payment (GCash Philippines)

#### 13.3 Informal Economy
- **Retail dominance**: traditional "mom-and-pop" shop vastly outnumber modern retail/e-commerce. In many markets >80% retail
- **Cash-based**: payment ecosystem cash-centric, credit/debit low penetration
- **Distribuzione fragmented**: wholesale → distributor → sub-distributor → retailer. Multi-tier, high margin erosion
- **Relationship-driven**: retailer decision based on trade credit, distributor relationship, personal connection vs brand marketing
- **Implicazioni**: trade marketing spend disproportionate vs consumer marketing. Sales force/distributor incentive critical. Promotional products important (gifts, premium, volume incentives)

### 14. International Marketing Research

#### 14.1 Data Equivalence Challenges (van de Vijver & Leung framework)
- **Construct equivalence**: il concetto esiste nello stesso modo entrambe culture? Ex. "price sensitivity" construct in honor culture vs shame culture differently manifestata
- **Scalar/measurement equivalence**: scala Likert 1-5 interpretata allo stesso modo? In power-distance culture, may avoid extreme responses
- **Linguistic equivalence**: parola-per-parola translation loses semantic meaning. "Good" ≠ "buono" connotativamente
- **Sample equivalence**: campione rappresentativo di target? Bias of sampling frame, non-response rate differisce culturalmente (privacy concerns Asia, trust issues LatAm)

#### 14.2 Cross-Cultural Research Methods
- **Emic approach**: local-specific dimensions, culturally unique phenomena (ex. guanxi China, kinsfolk Mexico)
- **Etic approach**: universal dimensions tested cross-culturally
- **Back-translation** (Brislin 1976): traduzione originale A→B language (traslatore 1) → back-translation B→A (traslatore 2 diverso) → verifica deviazione da originale. Identifica semantic drift
- **Culturally-sensitive recruiting**: local recruiters, appropriate channels (ex. ID via regione/clan/tribe vs centralized sampling), cultural timing (religious holidays, family farming season)
- **Qualitative depth**: focus group insight piu deep understanding cultural context vs survey quantitativo

#### 14.3 Comparability Cross-market
- Measurement invariance testing (structural equation model)
- Anchor vignettes (describe scenario, assess response) per control cultural response style
- Country cluster analysis (group similar cultures via CAGE/Hofstede) vs direct pairwise comparison

### 15. Global vs Regional vs Local Organizations

#### 15.1 Matrice Organizzativa (Lawrence & Lorsch framework)

| Dimensione | Centralized Global | Decentralized Local | Regional Hub | Transnational Network |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Decision locus | HQ global | Country-level autonome | Hub regional + country | Distributed per competence |
| Efficienza | High (scale) | Low (duplication) | Medium | High (specialization) |
| Local insight | Low | High | Medium | Medium-High |
| Brand consistency | High | Low | Medium | High |
| Speed | Slow | Fast | Medium | Fast |
| Complexity | Lower | High | Medium | Very high |
| Esempi | Pre-2000 Coke | Nestlé 1980s | Unilever | P&G, Microsoft |

#### 15.2 Decentralized Local
- Autonomia massima per country/market. Strategie diverse per mercato. Risk: duplicazione, inconsistenza brand, mancanza di scale economy
- Pro: rispetto culturale, speed to market, local accountability
- Contro: inefficienza, brand dilution, hard to coordinate global initiatives

#### 15.3 Centralized Global
- HQ decide strategie globali, paesi eseguono. Massima efficienza, brand consistency, scale economy
- Pro: efficienza, controllo, global reach uniformità
- Contro: lentezza, no local relevance, resistenza local market

#### 15.4 Regional Hub Model
- HQ + regional hub (EMEA HQ Berlin, APAC HQ Singapore, Americas HQ New York). Hub coordina region, country execute
- Pro: balance efficiency + local relevance, reduce HQ burden, regional expertise
- Contro: complexity, hub-country friction

#### 15.5 Transnational Network (Bartlett & Ghoshal)
- Capabilities distributed globally per specializzazione (ex. R&D Japan, Manufacturing Germany, Design Scandinavia, Call Center India). Collaborazione network cross-border
- Pro: world-class capability concentration, efficiency, innovation
- Contro: complexity massima, coordination overhead, cultural integration difficult

#### 15.6 Scelta Organizzativa Determinant
- **Product standardization**: high → centralized global. Low → decentralized local
- **Market diversity**: high → regional hub or transnational. Low → global centralized
- **Regulatory variance**: high → local/regional. Low → global
- **Cultural diversity**: high → local empowerment. Low → global
- **Capital efficiency goal**: maximize → centralized. Balanced growth → regional/transnational
