# Customer Journey Mapping

## Che cos'è una Customer Journey Map?

Una **Customer Journey Map** è una rappresentazione visuale e tabulare dei touchpoint, emotion, pain point, e opportunità che il cliente sperimenta nelle interazioni con un'organizzazione, dalle fasi di awareness fino all'advocacy.

**Scopo**:
- Identificare friction points che rallentano la customer experience
- Scoprire opportunità di personalizzazione e delight moments
- Allineare team (sales, marketing, service) su persona-specific tactics
- Progettare interventi data-driven (kampagne, content, training) per improve NPS/CSAT

---

## Step 1: Persona Development

### Anatomia di una Persona

Ogni persona deve includere:

#### A) Demographic Profile
- Nome (fittizio), age range, gender, income level
- Job title, industry, company size
- Location, education

**Esempio - Persona: "Giovanni, Marketing Manager a PMI"**
- Età: 35-45
- Titolo: Marketing Manager presso PMI manifatturiera (50-250 dipendenti)
- Reddito annuale: €40-60K
- Istruzione: Laurea in economia/marketing, MBA facoltativo
- Ubicazione: Milano, area metropolitana

#### B) Behavioral Profile
- Devices used: Desktop (60%), mobile (40%), tablet (10%)
- Online behavior: Search frequency, social media platform usage, email habits
- Purchase behavior: Impulse (20%) vs deliberate (80%), deal sensitivity
- Time constraint: Weekday morning (preferred), decision-making cycle

**Esempio per Giovanni**:
- Accede a LinkedIn 3x/settimana, ricerca "B2B marketing software" 1x/mese
- Apre email marketing > 9am (85% aperte se prima di 10am)
- Ciclo decisionale: 4-6 settimane (richiede multiple touchpoint)
- Deal sensitivity: alto (budget limitato, ROI-focused)

#### C) Psychographic Profile
- Values: Cost-efficiency, brand reputation, customer success story
- Pain points (key): Time scarcity (marketing team di 2 persone), lead quality (50% are not qualified), ROI justification to CEO
- Goals: Generate 100 qualified leads/month, reduce cost-per-lead by 25%, improve marketing-sales alignment
- Attitudes: Skeptical of new tools ("we tried 3 already"), prefers proven vendor, wants easy onboarding

#### D) Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)
Instead of "what do they look like?", ask "What job is the customer trying to accomplish?"

- **Functional job**: "I need to identify and nurture high-probability leads to feed my sales team"
- **Emotional job**: "I want to feel like a smart marketer delivering business results, not wasting budget"
- **Social job**: "I want my CEO & CFO to see me as strategic, not just a cost center"

| Dimensione | Giovanni (Mid-Market Marketer) | Sofia (E-Commerce Growth Lead) |
|-----------|---|---|
| **Demographic** | 39 anni, Milano, €50K salary, Laurea | 28 anni, Roma, €35K salary, bootcamp marketing |

| **Decision Maker?** | No (needs CEO/CFO approval for > €10K spend) | Yes (autonomous budget authority up to €5K/month) |
| **Sales Cycle** | 6-8 weeks | 2-3 weeks |

## Step 2: Journey Stage Definition

Standard CRM journey stages (applicable across e-commerce, SaaS, B2B, hospitality):

### Stage 1: Awareness
**Customer mindset**: "I have a problem/need I didn't know was solvable"

**Sample touchpoints**:
- Google search (organic result), social media ad, blog article, industry conference, referral
- Website homepage, product listing page, brand social media profile

**Key activity**:
- Consume educational content (buyer's guide, industry report, comparison)
- Follow brand on social, add to interest list

**CRM status**: Lead, not yet scored

**Typical duration**: 2-4 weeks (product awareness) or 6-12 months (category awareness)

---

### Stage 2: Consideration
**Customer mindset**: "I know the problem exists. Which solution is best for my specific situation?"

**Sample touchpoints**:
- Product demo, case study, webinar, email nurture sequence, sales consultation, product comparison page
- Review site (G2, Capterra for SaaS; Amazon for e-commerce; Trustpilot for service)
- Specification sheet, pricing page, free trial signup

**Key activity**:
- Request trial/demo, download content, compare multiple vendors
- Engage with sales team (respond to email, attend webinar)

**CRM status**: Marketing-qualified lead (MQL), scored based on engagement

**Typical duration**: 3-8 weeks

---

### Stage 3: Decision
**Customer mindset**: "I've chosen a vendor. Now I need to complete the transaction."

**Sample touchpoints**:
- Pricing conversation, contract negotiation, payment flow, order confirmation
- Post-purchase onboarding email, customer success call, welcome video

**Key activity**:
- Request pricing, negotiate terms, complete purchase
- Setup account, initial product training

**CRM status**: Sales-qualified lead (SQL) → Opportunity → Deal Won

**Typical duration**: 1-4 weeks

---

### Stage 4: Service / Adoption
**Customer mindset**: "I've bought the product. Am I using it correctly? Do I get the promised value?"

**Sample touchpoints**:
- In-app onboarding tutorial, email guidance (feature unlock), proactive outreach from customer success manager
- Support ticket, knowledge base article, community forum, webinar for power users
- Usage milestone celebration ("You've completed 10 projects!")

**Key activity**:
- Complete initial setup (60% of customers drop here if friction too high)
- Reach activation milestone (e.g., "ran first campaign", "uploaded first product catalog")
- Engage customer success team, attend training

**CRM status**: Customer, adoption stage tracked in analytics

**Typical duration**: 2-8 weeks to reach activation; ongoing adoption metrics

---

### Stage 5: Loyalty / Expansion
**Customer mindset**: "This product works great. Should I expand usage or buy additional products?"

**Sample touchpoints**:
- Upsell email (new feature, higher tier), customer success manager recommendation
- Expansion webinar, customer advisory board invitation, loyalty program tier promotion
- Renewal notice (contract coming due), expansion opportunity pipeline

**Key activity**:
- Upgrade to higher tier (SaaS), add additional users, buy adjacent product
- Provide positive reference, participate in customer marketing

**CRM status**: Customer with expansion revenue tracked, NPS survey sent

**Typical duration**: 3-12 months post-initial adoption

---

### Stage 6: Advocacy
**Customer mindset**: "I love this product. I want to recommend it to others."

**Sample touchpoints**:
- Referral program invitation, case study participation, testimonial request
- User conference attendance, community expert recognition, public review posting

**Key activity**:
- Write positive review (G2, Capterra, TrustRadius), refer other companies
- Participate in case study, attend user event

**CRM status**: VIP/Advocate segment in CRM

**Typical duration**: Ongoing

## Step 3: Touchpoint Mapping

### Touchpoint Classification

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Owned** | Channels you control | Your website, email, SMS, mobile app, CRM, sales team |
| **Paid** | Channels you buy access to | Google Ads, Facebook Ads, LinkedIn ads, sponsorship, affiliate, paid content syndication |
| **Earned** | Channels controlled by third parties | Social media mentions, press coverage, user reviews (G2), referrals, word-of-mouth |

### Journey Touchpoint Matrix (E-Commerce Example)

```
AWARENESS STAGE:
├── Owned: Blog article "Why customers choose us", Newsletter (organic subscribers)
├── Paid: Google Search (brand intent keywords), Facebook carousel ads (retargeting), LinkedIn sponsored content
└── Earned: Industry influencer mention, product review site listing, social media organic mentions

CONSIDERATION STAGE:
├── Owned: Product comparison page, feature explainer video (on website), email nurture sequence (3 emails over 2 weeks)
├── Paid: Google Search (comparison keywords: "Product X vs Product Y"), YouTube product demo ad
└── Earned: Independent product review (YouTuber), Reddit discussion, G2 reviews

DECISION STAGE:
├── Owned: Pricing calculator, checkout flow, cart abandonment email (1-3 hours post-abandon)
├── Paid: Retargeting ad to users who viewed pricing (show discount offer)
└── Earned: Social proof (star rating, review count displayed on product page)

SERVICE STAGE:
├── Owned: Post-purchase email (setup instructions, video tutorials), in-app welcome wizard, customer success check-in email (day 3)
├── Paid: Email campaign series (getting started tips, week 1 and week 3)
└── Earned: Product community forum (peer-to-peer support), user-generated content (how-to videos shared on YouTube)

LOYALTY STAGE:
├── Owned: Account dashboard (usage analytics, recommendations), email upsell (upgrade tier), loyalty program notification
├── Paid: Email winback campaign (to inactive users), retargeting to existing customers
└── Earned: User conference invitation, community expert recognition

ADVOCACY STAGE:
├── Owned: Referral program email ("Invite 3 friends, get benefit X"), testimonial request, case study collaboration
├── Paid: Affiliate commission communication
└── Earned: Social media organic post encouragement ("Share your success story"), review site encouragement
```

## Step 4: Emotion Mapping

### Emotion Framework

Map sentiment at each touchpoint using 3 emotions: Positive 😊, Neutral 😐, Negative 😞

**Positive emotions** indicate moments of delight, trust, confidence.
**Neutral emotions** indicate routine transactional interactions.
**Negative emotions** indicate friction, frustration, confusion, distrust.

### Example Journey Map with Emotions

**Persona**: Giovanni, Mid-Market B2B Marketer | **Journey Stage**: Awareness → Consideration

| Touchpoint | Emotion | Why | Insight |
|-----------|---------|-----|---------|
| Search "B2B lead generation software" | 😞 | Overwhelmed by 50+ results, unclear differentiation | SEO opportunity: target long-tail keywords like "B2B lead gen for small teams" |
| Click Google Ad ("Generate 100 leads/month") | 😊 | Headline speaks directly to goal | Ad copy resonates; landing page should reinforce "small team efficiency" |
| Landing page with form | 😞 | 15 required fields; feels invasive; bounces | Reduce form fields to 3 (email, company, pain point); offer downloadable guide without form capture |
| Receives welcome email series | 😐 | Expected transactional email; skims for content | Opportunity: add customer success story (day 2) showing similar company's results → move to 😊 |
| Invited to webinar "Lead Gen ROI" | 😊 | Topic directly relevant, timing convenient | High webinar attendance probability; follow-up sales call should close 20-30% |
| No follow-up for 2 weeks post-webinar | 😞 | Interested but forgotten; moves to competitor | Automation issue: design immediate post-webinar nurture sequence (email day 0, day 3, day 7) |

### Emotion Heatmap (Visual Format)

```
Awareness     Consideration     Decision     Service     Loyalty
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
😞 Search      😊 Demo           😐 Form     😊 Onboard  😞 Lack of
😊 Ad click    😞 Pricing shock  😊 Confirm  😐 Email     expansion
😐 Landing     😊 Case study     😞 Slow     😊 Success  😊 Referral
              😐 Email series          fulfillment   call     ask
```

**Insight**: Biggest emotional dips are: Pricing page (consideration), Post-purchase setup friction (service). Design interventions to improve these.

## Step 5: Pain Points Identification

### Pain Point Analysis Template

| Pain Point | Stage | Severity (H/M/L) | Root Cause | Impact | Solution |
|-----------|-------|---------|-----------|--------|----------|
| Overwhelmed by search results | Awareness | M | Too many competitors, unclear positioning | Longer consideration stage, competitor browsing | Create comparison content (us vs. 3 key competitors) |
| 15-field form deters signup | Awareness → Consideration | H | Legacy marketing automation form requirements | 40% form abandonment rate | Progressive profiling: 3 fields on landing, remaining fields via email nurture |
| Pricing not transparent | Consideration | H | Sales-driven pricing model (custom quotes) | Extended sales cycle, prospect shops around | Add pricing calculator online, show standard packages + enterprise option |

| No account manager → no upsell | Loyalty | M | Cost (hiring sales/CS team is expensive) | Only 10% annual expansion revenue vs 30% target | Create self-serve expansion (in-app recommendation engine), plus automated upsell email |
| Renewal notice arrives 30 days before | Loyalty | L | Contract management system issue | Risk of surprise cancellation | Change notice to 90 days out; add value recaps quarterly |

## Step 6: Moments of Truth (Jan Carlzon Framework)

### Four Moments of Truth (4MoT)

**Zero Moment of Truth (ZMOT)**: Pre-purchase research
- Customer researches problem online (search, reviews, social media)
- Your brand presence: SEO visibility, review ratings, social proof
- **Action**: Invest in SEO for intent keywords, maintain 4.5+ star ratings, active social presence

**First Moment of Truth (1MoT)**: Shelf/storefront/initial product experience
- Customer visits your store, website, or opens product for first time
- Typically 5-10 seconds to capture interest
- **Action**: Mobile-responsive design, fast page load (< 2 seconds), clear value proposition above the fold

**Second Moment of Truth (2MoT)**: Actual product usage
- Customer uses product at home / office, evaluates against competitor
- Determines whether it delivers promised value
- **Action**: Frictionless onboarding, early success metric (activation within 1 week), proactive customer success outreach

**Third Moment of Truth (3MoT)**: Sharing & advocacy
- Customer shares experience (review, social post, referral, word-of-mouth)
- This becomes someone else's ZMOT
- **Action**: Referral program, easy social sharing, encourage positive reviews

### Example: SaaS CRM Tool

```
ZMOT: Prospect Googles "Best CRM for SMB"
  → You rank #3 on Google (SEO opportunity)
  → Your G2 profile shows 4.6 stars, 500+ reviews (trust builder)
  → Social proof: "Trusted by 5,000+ SMBs" on homepage

1MoT: Prospect lands on your website
  → Page loads in 1.2 seconds (mobile-optimized)
  → Headline: "Sales pipeline management for teams under 20 people"
  → CTA: "Try free 14-day trial" (low friction entry)
  → Time to first sign-up: 90 seconds

2MoT: User completes sign-up & logs in
  → Meets in-app welcome wizard (video: "Complete setup in 5 minutes")
  → Pre-loaded sample data (customers, pipeline, activities)
  → Day 1: User creates first opportunity (activation milestone)
  → Day 3: Customer success email "You've set up your first opportunity! Here are 3 next steps"
  → Day 14: Renewal email "Upgrade to premium to get 25 users instead of 5"

3MoT: Customer becomes advocate
  → Writes review on G2 ("This CRM saved us 5 hours/week on admin work")
  → Refers 2 colleagues (via referral link in app)
  → Invites 1 colleague to write case study
```

## Step 7: Service Blueprint

### Concept

A **Service Blueprint** extends the customer journey map by adding:
- **Line of visibility**: What customer sees vs. what happens backstage
- **Onstage activities**: Customer-facing interactions
- **Backstage activities**: Support processes invisible to customer
- **Support processes**: Technology, HR, training required

### Service Blueprint Template: E-Commerce Checkout

```
CUSTOMER ACTIONS:
  Add item → View cart → Enter checkout → Enter payment → Confirm order → Receive confirmation email

LINE OF VISIBILITY (←────────────────────→)

ONSTAGE (Customer-facing):
  Product page    | Shopping cart review  | Shipping form | Payment form | Order confirmation
  "Add to cart"   | "Proceed to checkout" | Address fields| Card fields  | "Order #123456"
  button          | button                | Validate      | Process      | email sent

BACKSTAGE:
  Inventory       | Cart system checks    | Address        | Payment      | Order fulfillment
  system updates  | promo code validity   | verification   | processor    | system gets order
  availability    | customer history      | via USPS API   | (Stripe)     | confirmation email

SUPPORT PROCESS / ENABLING SYSTEMS:
  Product info    | CRM (customer history)| Shipping system| PCI-DSS      | Email system
  database        | Discount system       | integration    | compliance   | (Sendgrid)
  (Shopify)       | (Salesforce Commerce) | (ShipStation)  | (Square)     | Order tracking API

SUPPORT PROCESS / PEOPLE:
  Category mgmt   | Customer service      | Warehouse      | Fraud team   | Operations team
  team            | (chat, email)         | staff          | (manual      | (morning report
  updates         | (if customer help     | (pick & pack)  | review if    | on failed orders)
  inventory       | needed)               |                | threshold)   |
```

### Key Insight from Service Blueprint
- **Gap 1**: Customer may expect "fast checkout" but backstage payment processing takes 2-3 seconds, creating anxiety. **Solution**: Optimistic UI (show "order confirmed" while processing in background)
- **Gap 2**: Address validation might fail (e.g., apartment without unit number). **Solution**: Allow customer to override or call support (human backstage process)

## Step 8: Omnichannel vs Multichannel vs Unified Commerce

### Definitions

| Model | Definition | Example | Customer Experience |
|-------|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Multichannel** | Multiple independent channels | Customer buys on website, returns in-store; customer service channel separate from sales | Customer must repeat context in each channel (painful) |
| **Omnichannel** | Channels integrated behind the scenes; unified data but separate experiences per channel | Customer browses on mobile, buys on desktop, returns in-store; all tied to same customer record | Seamless, but each channel has separate UI/UX optimized for device |
| **Unified Commerce** | Single unified experience; inventory, pricing, customer data real-time across all touchpoints | Customer adds to cart on mobile, completes purchase on desktop (cart persists), can pickup in-store same day, returns via mail | True "one customer, one experience" – highest complexity, highest customer satisfaction |

### Omnichannel Architecture Example

```
CUSTOMER DATA UNIFIED
     ↓
[Master Customer Record in CDP/CRM]
     ↓
TOUCHPOINTS (Each optimized for device, but unified data):
├── Web (Desktop) → Browse catalog → Add to cart → Checkout → Track order
├── Mobile App → Browse → Buy → Track → Get notifications
├── In-Store → Kiosk (check inventory online) → POS (see customer purchase history) → Recommend based on browsing
├── Call Center → Agent sees full customer history (browsing, purchases, support tickets) → Personalized service
└── Social Commerce → Instagram Shopping → Checkout → Order confirmation

INVENTORY UNIFIED
└── 3 items in warehouse → Allocate based on order priority → Real-time view across channels
```

## Step 10: Data-Driven Journey Optimization

### Journey Mining Workflow

**Journey mining** = sequence analysis to discover common paths customers take and where they drop off.

**Example data**:
```
User A path: Search → Click Google Ad → View homepage → Watch 2 product demos → Download guide → Schedule call → → → DEAL WON
           (2 days)  (5 min)           (1 min)        (15 min)           (2 days)         (7 days)            (14 days)

User B path: Search → Search again → Click Ad → Click ad → View pricing → Bounce
           (1 week)  (2 days)       (15 min)  (next day)  (5 min)

User C path: Search → Search again → Email signup → 0 opens → Unsubscribe → LOST
           (2 days)  (1 week)       (signup form) (over 3 weeks)
```

**Analysis**:
- Common pattern: Users who download guide have 5x higher conversion rate
- Dropout pattern: Users who view pricing without context bounce (show pricing in demo instead)
- Email inefficiency: 50% unsubscribe rate in nurture sequence (revise email content)

**Micro-Journey Optimization** = identify smallest high-impact interventions

| Micro-Journey | Current Conversion | Issue | Intervention | Expected Lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage → Product demo click | 35% | CTA button below fold (6th scroll) | Move CTA above fold (2nd scroll) | +15% |
| Demo completion → Download guide | 20% | No immediate CTA after demo | Add "Get your personalized guide" prompt post-demo | +30% |
| Guide download → Sales email open | 15% | Generic "We'll follow up" email | Personalized email based on company size from form | +40% |
| Sales email open → Call scheduled | 5% | Call CTA too passive ("if you'd like to discuss") | Add urgency ("slots filling fast") + reduce friction (calendar link) | +50% |

**18-month optimization roadmap**:
- Q1: Fix homepage CTA placement (+15% funnel efficiency)
- Q2: Post-demo prompt engineering (+30% guide downloads)
- Q3: Email personalization (company size, industry) (+40% open/response)
- Q4: Meeting scheduling UX (calendar integration) (+50% conversion)
- **Cumulative funnel improvement**: 35% → 50% (43% lift overall)

## Journey Mapping Checklist

- [ ] **Personas**: Defined 3-5 primary personas with demographic, behavioral, JTBD (Jobs-to-be-Done)
- [ ] **Stages**: Mapped awareness → consideration → decision → service → loyalty → advocacy
- [ ] **Touchpoints**: Listed owned/paid/earned channels per stage
- [ ] **Emotions**: Assigned sentiment (😊 😐 😞) to each touchpoint; identified "low points"
- [ ] **Pain Points**: Documented friction (form abandonment, pricing shock, setup complexity) with root cause
- [ ] **Moments of Truth**: Identified ZMOT (research phase), 1MoT (first impression), 2MoT (product experience), 3MoT (advocacy)
- [ ] **Service Blueprint**: Created backstage/onstage/support process diagram for key stage (e.g., checkout)
- [ ] **Omnichannel Integration**: Planned how channels share customer data (CDP, unified profile)
- [ ] **Analytics Setup**: Configured journey tracking (GA4, Mixpanel, Amplitude) to measure real drop-off rates
- [ ] **Optimization Roadmap**: Prioritized micro-journeys with highest impact-to-effort ratio for Q1-Q4
- [ ] **Stakeholder Sign-Off**: Sales, marketing, service, product aligned on journey priorities

---

**Key Takeaway**: A well-designed journey map reduces customer acquisition cost by 20-30%, improves conversion rate by 10-25%, and increases NPS by 10+ points through targeted friction reduction and delight moment creation.
